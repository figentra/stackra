## Context

A tenant sells in several countries, and each one needs its own currency, price list, tax rules, payment methods, shipping and language. Those rules were spread across pricing, tax and payments. The owner's market answer (mem L888, 2026-10-20) puts them in one entity, a Market, owned by pricing.

## Decision

1. **The entity.** A Market groups countries, and carries the currency, the price list, the tax rules, the payment methods and processors, the shipping zones, the language, and the domain or sub-folder.
2. **Price lists.** A price list is explicit, or adjusted from a base list (for example, a purchasing-power adjustment).
3. **Resolution.** A visitor is matched to a Market by the domain, by the address, or by geo-IP, in that order of precedence.
4. **Readers.** Pricing, commerce, billing, payments, tax, shipping and localisation read the Market. None of them keeps its own copy.
5. **Multiple markets.** More than one Market is an entitlement.
6. **Rounding.** Prices round to a price point, per Market.
7. **Data region.** The Market does not change the data region, which stays a tenancy property (ADR 0210).
8. **Own plans.** Figentra uses Markets for its own plans (ADR 0242).

## Consequences

- A change to a Market's tax or currency is made in one place, and every reader sees it.
- The data region and the selling market are separate, so a Market in one country does not move a tenant's data.

## Enforcement

planned — the resolution test covers each of the three matching methods; the readers' contract test checks they read the Market and do not copy it.

## Open questions

1. Which entity decides the currency and the tax when a tenant's market and the end customer's address differ (register D-15). The owner decides.
