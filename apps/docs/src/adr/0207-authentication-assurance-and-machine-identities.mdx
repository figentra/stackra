## Context

Some actions need a stronger proof of identity than a session. The owner's answers I5, I6 and SC14 (mem L609, L610, L553, 2026-10-20) fix the step-up rule, the machine identity types, and the ASVS baseline.

## Decision

1. **MFA policy (I5).** An MFA policy cascades down the tenant tree. The authentication assurance level (AAL) is carried in the token. A level L3 or L4 action needs a step-up within 10 minutes. Without it, the refusal is `step_up_required`.
2. **Machine identities (I6).** All machine identities go through ZITADEL: service accounts, personal access tokens (free), tenant service tokens (an entitlement tier), OAuth apps, and the device flow for devices (ADR 0235).
3. **Baseline (SC14, ASVS level 1).** MFA for administrators and operators. A breached-password check. Sessions last 30 days, or 12 hours on the platform. Rate limits and Turnstile.

## Consequences

- A destructive action cannot run on an old session, because a step-up is required within the window.
- Tenant service tokens are a plan tier, so a machine credential is part of the commercial model (ADR 0242).

## Enforcement

planned — the pipeline checks the AAL claim against the action's level (ADR 0138); the validator checks that every L3 action declares its step-up.

## Open questions

1. The ASVS level for SaaS general availability: gap analysis P1-1 proposes L2 for identity, sessions and access control. The owner decides.
