## Context

Money is wrong in a payment system when it is stored as a float, rounded differently in two places, or changed in place. The owner's money answers PP3, PP8, MN1 and P2 (mem L517, L797, L872, 2026-10-20) fix the representation, the rounding and the ledger shape.

## Decision

1. **Packages.** `@stackra/money` (commerce category) represents money in minor units with ISO 4217 currency codes and an `@Money` field. A ledger package records every movement. FX is snapshotted at the transaction time. Each tenant has a base currency.
2. **Exponent (MN1).** The ISO 4217 exponent is used, so KWD and BHD have three digits and JPY has none.
3. **Rounding (MN1).** Half-even rounding by default. Half-up where a tax rule requires it. Allocation of a ratio is exact, with deterministic remainders. FX stores the rate and its source.
4. **Ledger (P2).** A double-entry, immutable ledger: accounts per party and purpose, balances derived from entries, and reversals instead of edits.
5. **Idempotency.** Money idempotency keys stay in Postgres, in the same entry as the movement (restated from 0036).

## Consequences

- A balance is always the sum of entries, so a mistake is corrected by a reversal that remains visible.
- Rounding is decided once, so two services cannot disagree on a cent.

## Enforcement

planned — property tests on the allocation and the rounding modes; the ledger rejects an update to a posted entry.
