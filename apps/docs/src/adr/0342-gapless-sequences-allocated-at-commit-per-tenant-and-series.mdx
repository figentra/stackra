## Context

Invoices, receipts, legal filings and ledger entries need numbers that have no gaps and are never reused, per tenant and
per series, and tax authorities check the gaps. A Postgres `SEQUENCE` is not gapless: a rolled-back transaction burns a
number. Products have each written their own counter table, and a retail edition also needs numbers for documents
issued on an offline till and submitted later. The owner locked the legal product's plan with this need on 2026-10-24,
and the retail edition's e-receipts need the same primitive.

## Decision

1. **A `Sequence` primitive in `@stackra/database`.** A module declares a sequence as a class that extends
   `Sequence({ key })` in its `database/sequences/` group. Its scope is the tenant plus the series dimensions the module
   names (`series: ["legalEntityId", "locationId"]`), and its format is a pattern (`"INV-{year}-{n:06}"`) with a reset
   rule (`never`, `yearly`).

2. **Allocated at commit.** A number is taken inside the transaction that writes the document, from a per-scope counter
   row locked for the transaction, so a rolled-back write takes no number and two commits never share one. The
   allocation is the last step before commit, so the lock is held briefly.

3. **Never reused.** A document's number is immutable. A cancelled document keeps its number and is cancelled by a
   later document that references it; nothing renumbers.

4. **Reservation blocks for offline issue.** A device that issues documents offline
   ([0338](/adr/0338-offline-queue-completed-per-device-order-sealed-payloads-bounded-replicas-revoke-and-caps)) holds a
   reserved block per series (`reserve: { size }`), taken at sync time; the documents it issues take numbers from the
   block in order, and an unused remainder is returned on the next sync or, when a device is wiped, recorded as voided
   numbers with the reason, so the gap is explained and audited.

5. **One primitive, no per-product counters.** A module never writes its own counter table. A product that needs a
   series the primitive cannot express extends the primitive in `@stackra/database`, with an ADR.

## Consequences

- A tax audit of a series finds every number, in order, each either a document or an explained void.
- Offline tills issue legal numbers without the network, and the audit trail says which block they came from.
- A hot series serialises on its counter row for the duration of the commit; a product that needs more throughput adds
  a series dimension, not a second counter.

## Enforcement

- planned — a conformance test in `@stackra/database`: concurrent commits on one series produce consecutive numbers, a
  rolled-back commit leaves no gap, and a wiped device's unused block appears as voids.
- planned — gen (STA-340): a model field marked as a document number without a `Sequence` fails; a migration that
  creates a Postgres `SEQUENCE` for a document number fails.
