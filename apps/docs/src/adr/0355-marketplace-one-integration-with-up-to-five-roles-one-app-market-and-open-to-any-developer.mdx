## Context

[0218](/adr/0218-integrations-the-connector-sdk-and-connections-per-chain-level) decision 5 left the marketplace model to
Figentra OS. [0308](/adr/0308-integrations-v3-1-provider-descriptions-connection-stores-and-webhooks) rule 21 put
third-party publishers out of scope. Nothing said how one provider that plays several parts (Slack is a chat channel, an
app surface and a workflow trigger) is described once, or which providers a tenant sees in the market. First-party
modules ([0201](/adr/0201-the-app-split-framework-platform-core-and-first-party-apps) decision 3) and partner apps had no
shared install and billing path. The owner answered these on 2026-10-25: the round-3 integration questions 5 to 7 and
11, and the OS questions OS-127, OS-128, OS-129, OS-217 and OS-382, with the round-9 pricing answer.

## Decision

**One provider, one Integration (round-3 #5).**

1. A provider is one Integration: one description, one Connection shape and one webhook route
   ([0308](/adr/0308-integrations-v3-1-provider-descriptions-connection-stores-and-webhooks) rules 1, 2 and 15). It plays
   one or more of five roles, declared on the Integration so the listing can show them:
   - **driver**: a capability we own calls it through a port (send a message, charge a card, store a file, clear an
     e-invoice). The port belongs to the package or OS service that owns the capability.
   - **channel**: a driver behind one notifications transport (mail, sms, whatsapp, push, inApp, chat, voice).
     Products declare channels, never providers
     ([0223](/adr/0223-notifications-channels-cascading-templates-manual-sends-and-preferences) decision 2).
   - **surface**: its host shows our tools, commands or cards
     ([0348](/adr/0348-integration-surfaces-assistant-hosts-chat-apps-messaging-flows-and-wallet-passes)).
   - **connector**: it holds records we sync or starts our workflows, with triggers, actions and syncs
     ([0218](/adr/0218-integrations-the-connector-sdk-and-connections-per-chain-level) decision 3).
   - **webhooks**: it sends us events, as `WebhookEvent<I>` on the same Integration, never a second route.
2. **Listed only when the tenant is involved.** An Integration is listed in the app market only if a tenant connects
   it, consents to it or pays for it. An Integration whose only credential is the platform's has a platform Connection
   and no listing.
3. **Slack (round-3 #6)** is one Integration: the driver of the `chat` channel, the Slack app surface, and workflow
   triggers and actions.
4. **WhatsApp (round-3 #7)** is its own channel transport, not a variant of SMS.
5. **Ad and social platforms (Commerce answers, Channels).** Meta, TikTok, Snap and Google are one Integration each,
   covering the shop, the catalogue feed and tracking.
6. **Muslin's connector catalogue (round-3 #11)** becomes shared Integration listings; it is not kept as a second
   catalogue.

**One app market (round 9).**

7. One app market holds first-party modules, partner apps and connector listings: one install flow, one bill, and an
   entitlement per module. A listing's price stays a reference to an Entitlement (0308 rule 21).
8. **Connectors at launch (OS-217).** Only first-party connectors ship at launch. Most are included in the product
   plan. Premium connectors (ERP, accounting, marketplaces, sales channels) are paid. Sync runs are metered. Partner
   connectors come later.

**Developers and partners.**

9. **Open to any developer (OS-127).** Anyone can create an app, integrate it and test it in developer sandboxes. A
   Figentra review team approves an app before it is listed. Developer sandboxes, the review process and the developer
   portal are launch scope.
10. **Billing partner apps (OS-128).** Figentra bills the tenant for a partner app on its one invoice and pays the
    partner monthly.
11. **Fee schedules (OS-129).** Sales sets a fee schedule per partner: tiered, flat percentage, percentage with a cap,
    fixed per install, or custom. The default is 0% on a partner's first 1M USD, then 15%. Every schedule and every
    change is audited and needs a second approver.
12. **Agencies (OS-382).** An agency programme ships at launch: delegated access to client tenants and a referral
    share (for example 20% for 12 months). Resellers come later.

**Settled by their recommended default (owner rule, `default-applied.md`).**

13. Tenant admins install modules per tenant; organisation admins may force-install or allow-list apps for child
    tenants (OS-122). Minor and patch versions auto-update; a major version or new permissions wait for admin consent
    (OS-123). Uninstall keeps the module's data 30 days, then deletes it with notice; legal holds win (OS-124). A
    disabled paid module is not billed from the next period (OS-125).
14. A listing may be free or flat monthly at launch; per seat and usage pricing come later (OS-130). Manual review for
    the first publish and for any scope or data-class change; automated checks for other versions (OS-131).
15. Private (unlisted) apps run in production on Enterprise; any tier may install its own unlisted app in a sandbox
    (OS-132). Partner apps may not request sensitive or privileged data classes at launch
    ([0341](/adr/0341-product-defined-data-classes-and-the-privileged-class), OS-133).
16. One developer portal for every product, with per-region endpoints (OS-136). On-prem installs take only approved,
    Figentra-signed app bundles from a customer allow-list (OS-141).

## Consequences

- A provider is described and listed once, whatever it does. Slack and Meta are not three listings each.
- The market shows a tenant only what the tenant connects, consents to or pays for. Platform plumbing stays out of it.
- One install flow and one invoice serve first-party modules and partner apps alike.
- Launch scope grows: developer sandboxes, a review queue with staff, the developer portal, partner payouts and per-partner
  fee schedules must exist before the first partner app is listed.
- The Activepieces piece format stays out of scope (0308 rule 21); only the publisher restriction changes.

## Enforcement

planned — a `roles` declaration on the Integration (or derived by gen from the classes that bind it), checked by gen
(STA-339/340); the listing schema in `@stackra/integrations` (today `IntegrationListing` holds only `docsUrl`); the OS
registry and integrations services for the install flow, review queue and partner payouts; the fee-schedule
second-approver check in OS billing.

## Open questions

1. **Partner apps versus partner connectors.** OS-127 opens app publishing to any developer; OS-217 keeps partner
   connectors for later. May a partner app declare its own Integration (its own provider credentials and webhooks) at
   launch, or only use first-party Integrations? Not decided.
2. **Shelves.** Whether connector listings and apps are separate shelves inside the one app market is not decided.
3. **Agency referral share.** "20% for 12 months" was given as an example in OS-382, not as the fixed rate.
