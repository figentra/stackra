## Context

Staff and customers belong to tenants, and a support engineer sometimes needs to act inside a customer's tenant. The owner's answers (mem L596–602, T4 L634, C-03, 2026-10-20) split membership from grants, and split support access across four services so no one service can do it alone.

## Decision

1. **Membership.** Tenancy owns membership: invitations, switching and the membership states. IAM owns the grants. Removing a membership emits an event, and IAM removes that member's grants.
2. **Support access, split (L597–602).**
   - IAM issues a time-boxed, revocable support grant.
   - Identity exchanges tokens (ZITADEL token exchange) with an "acting as" marker, and only while the grant exists.
   - A rotating support PIN verifies the support engineer.
   - The support service orchestrates the flow.
   - The audit and security-centre services record and alert.
3. **Limits.** The support mechanism keeps the limits of the earlier design: 30 minutes, read-only, audited, and the user is notified. This replaces the Better Auth admin plugin of PP14 (contradiction C-03).
4. **Actor.** The actor gains the acting-as marker, so every audit record says who acted for whom.

## Consequences

- No single service can grant itself access to a customer.
- A customer sees every support session, because the user is always notified.

## Enforcement

planned — the grant expiry is enforced by IAM; the acting-as marker is checked by the audit tests.
