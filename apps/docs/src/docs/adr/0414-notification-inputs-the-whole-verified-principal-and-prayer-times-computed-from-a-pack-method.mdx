## Context

Notifications sends email, SMS and WhatsApp and keeps quiet hours. `IdentityResolver` answered only a verified email
and a locale, so the service had no verified number to text and no time zone for quiet hours. Quiet hours in Muslim
markets also fall around prayer times, and no package computed them.

## Decision

1. **The whole principal.** `IdentityResolver.principalOf(principalId)` returns the `VerifiedPrincipal`, which now
   carries the verified `phone` (E.164) and the `timeZone` (IANA) beside `email` and `locale`. Both are optional: an
   unverified number is never returned, and a principal without a zone of their own leaves the caller to fall back to
   the tenant's or the location's. Nothing is copied into the caller's tables (ADR 0134). `@stackra/identity`'s drivers
   implement the new method.
2. **Prayer times are computed, the method is data.** `@stackra/calendar` has a `PrayerTimes` port and a computed
   driver (sun position, the praytimes.org method). The method is the pack key `calendar.prayer_times`: the Fajr angle,
   Isha as an angle or minutes after Maghrib, the Asr factor (1 or 2), minutes added to sunset for Maghrib, and a
   high-latitude rule (`none`, `angle_based`, `middle_of_night`, `one_seventh`). No authority's angles are written in
   code; a market or a tenant's location picks them in its pack. A driver over a published table can replace the
   computed one where a market requires the authority's own timetable.
3. **Instants out.** A day's times are UTC instants rounded to the minute; the caller formats them in the place's zone.

## Consequences

- Every `IdentityResolver` implementation adds `principalOf`.
- Packs for markets with prayer-aware quiet hours need a `calendar.prayer_times` entry; without one the calendar
  refuses rather than guesses.
