## Context

Stackra releases every `@stackra/*` package at one version
([0453](/adr/0453-the-stackra-registry-one-npm-compatible-store-on-cloudflare-and-our-own-licence-engine)
decision 18), and before `1.0.0` a minor release may break an API. The record on `main` shows what that means for a
buyer: 46 release pull requests ("version packages") merged from 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-11, fourteen of them on one day
(2026-11-06), and 33 minor versions from `0.1.0` to `0.33.1`. `0.33.1` followed `0.33.0` the next day as a set of about
twenty fixes that a product needed to build and boot on it (#1039). Nothing says when "any minor may break" ends,
which versions still get fixes, or how long a major lives after `1.0`; the support page lists all of these as owner
placeholders (STA-1576).

What already holds: every breaking release carries a written note ([0013](/adr/0013-versioning-and-release)
decision 5, carried by [0025](/adr/0025-packages-publish-to-private-registry)); a deprecation lasts one minor before
`1.0` and one major after, and long-term support starts at the first on-prem contract
([0177](/adr/0177-package-distribution-npmjs-and-keygen) decision 6); `@stackra-pro/*` releases at the same version as
`@stackra/*` (0453 decision 18). The free and Pro line is decided:
[0461](/adr/0461-the-pro-line-for-0-1-0-runtime-free-behind-entitlements-paid-studios-and-advanced-drivers) keeps every
runtime free behind plan entitlements and puts seven new packages (four studios, three drivers) in `@stackra-pro`, and
[0469](/adr/0469-stackra-ships-no-heroui-pro-free-heroui-and-stackras-own-components-only) ships no HeroUI Pro. What a
Pro licence holds besides those packages (support, long-term support, the unit it is sold by) is not decided.

The review of 2026-11-20 (STA-2705) proposed a weekly minor with patch-only releases between and one upgrade note per
minor. The owner accepted its test and consumer-gate parts; the cadence is still open. The work that makes a stable
line possible is in flight, not merged: tests in the Quality Gate (#1111), the consumer gate before publish (#1120,
#1125), the release-note check and upgrade notes (STA-2835), `stackra upgrade` with codemods (STA-145), the
`@Deprecated` marker (STA-2836) and the staged refusals turned into errors (STA-2808).

## Decision

Recommended, for the ruling.

1. **A weekly release train before 1.0.** One minor release a week, cut on Tuesday from `main` through the version
   pull request. Between two minors only patch releases ship, and a patch only fixes: it adds no export, removes or
   narrows nothing, and changes no public type. A security fix may ship as a patch on any day.
2. **Breaking changes wait for the breaking minor.** Before `1.0`, a breaking change ships only in the first minor of
   a calendar month (the breaking minor). Every other minor only adds. A breaking minor is announced on the changelog
   page one week before it ships, with its upgrade note, and a mechanical break ships with a `stackra upgrade` codemod.
   A security fix that cannot avoid a break may ship in the next minor outside the window, with its note.
3. **Fixes before 1.0.** Fixes land on the latest minor only; the fix for an older minor is to upgrade. For four weeks
   after a breaking minor, a security fix is also released on the last version before it, so an app that cannot take
   the break yet stays safe.
4. **The 1.0 bar.** `1.0.0` ships when every criterion below holds, checked by the release lead and recorded on the
   release's Linear issue. The date is not fixed in advance; the owner may publish a target quarter, and the changelog
   page says which criteria are still open.
   1. The Quality Gate runs tests and is a required check, and `main` has been green for four consecutive weeks.
   2. The consumer gate blocks a release, and the scaffold app plus at least two Figentra products pass it on every
      release of those four weeks.
   3. One Figentra product has run in production on a Stackra release for 30 days with no incident caused by Stackra.
   4. Each published package marks its public entry points, and a committed API report per package makes a breaking
      change fail CI unless its changeset is `major`.
   5. No staged refusal is left (`STAGED_REFUSAL_CODES` is empty), and no versioning or release rule of this ADR or
      0013, 0177 and 0453 is still `planned`.
   6. Every breaking minor since `0.30` has an upgrade note, and `stackra upgrade` runs the codemods between any
      two minors of that range.
   7. `stackra create` makes a working app from the published starter on Linux and macOS, proven in CI.
   8. The support terms on the customer support page are decided: no "owner to supply" row is left.
   9. The last two breaking minors before `1.0` shipped no breaking change (a quiet period of about two months).
5. **After 1.0: semantic versioning in full, two majors a year at most.** A minor only adds and a patch only fixes. A
   major ships at most twice a year, in fixed months chosen by the owner, announced at least eight weeks ahead with its
   upgrade note and codemods. A deprecated API keeps working for one major and at least six months (0177 decision 6
   stands; the six months are new).
6. **The support window after 1.0.** The latest major gets features and fixes. The previous major gets security and
   critical fixes for 12 months after the next major ships, then it reaches end of life; the end-of-life date is
   published on the support page when the next major ships. Long-term support stays a Pro term (decision 7).
7. **What a Pro licence adds.** The `@stackra-pro/*` packages are the 0461 list, unchanged for `1.0` (the line is
   decided; this ADR does not reopen it), and they release on the same day at the same version as `@stackra/*`. A Pro
   licence also carries: a private support channel with response times by severity; long-term support, where one
   major a year is named LTS and gets security fixes for 24 months; and the licence for on-prem and air-gapped
   installs. It is sold per organization, as an annual licence with a fixed number of live tokens (the registry
   already holds up to 20 per licence). The price, the response times and how to buy are the owner's to supply.

| When 1.0 ships           | (a) a fixed date; (b) when the criteria hold, with a target quarter; (c) when the first enterprise contract needs it                               | (b), decision 4: a date can force a 1.0 that still breaks; a target quarter gives buyers a horizon            |

## Consequences

- A buyer can plan: one possible break a month before `1.0`, announced a week ahead, and a published bar for `1.0`.
- The release lead's chore: cut the Tuesday minor, keep breaking changesets off `main` until the breaking minor (or on a
  `next` prerelease), and keep the 1.0 criteria list current on the changelog page.
- Breaking work queues for up to a month. A break that cannot wait ships as a `next` prerelease first.
- The 1.0 bar needs work that is not built yet: API reports per package (criterion 4) and a release-train check
  (Enforcement below).
- Pro gains terms that need people: a support rota and LTS backports. The owner decides the price and the response
  times before the support page can state them.

## Enforcement

Nothing checks this yet; it is a proposal.

- planned — a release-train check in `@stackra/repo-checks`, run by `pnpm validate`, refuses a changeset that the
  release-note check reads as breaking (STA-2835) outside the breaking-minor window, and a non-patch changeset on a
  patch release.
- planned — `.github/workflows/release.yml` opens the version pull request on Tuesday only for a minor; patches publish
  as today.
- planned — an API report per published package (criterion 4), compared in the Quality Gate.
- The release-note check (STA-2835), the consumer gate (STA-2705) and the `@Deprecated` marker (STA-2836) are the
  checks for decisions 2, 4 and 5 once they land.
