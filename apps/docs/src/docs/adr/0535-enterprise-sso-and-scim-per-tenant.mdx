## Context

Enterprise buyers ask for three things before they sign: their staff sign in with the company's own identity provider
(Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace, any SAML 2.0 or OIDC provider), accounts are created and removed from that provider
by SCIM 2.0, and the provider's groups decide the roles people hold. Stackra has none of the three: `git grep -i -E
'saml|scim' -- packages` on 2026-11-26 finds only the entitlement keys `access.sso` and `access.scim` in the settings
package's tests. The audit of 2026-11-26 lists this as the top enterprise blocker.

What already stands:

- **Where the IdP lives.** ZITADEL is the only identity provider and `@stackra/identity` is provider-neutral
  ([0206](/adr/0206-zitadel-is-the-identity-provider-and-stackra-identity-is-provider-neutral)). The owner decided on
  2026-11-08 that an Enterprise merchant's staff may sit in a dedicated staff organisation with its own SAML or OIDC IdP,
  enforced for its verified domains, with SCIM later ([0361](/adr/0361-principals-and-parties) and
  [0372](/adr/0372-national-digital-id-sign-in-as-zitadel-external-identity-providers), amendments of 2026-11-08).
- **Core or Pro.** [0077](/adr/0077-open-core-free-and-pro-lists) and
  [0087](/adr/0087-identity-and-iam-are-driver-packages) put SSO and SCIM in Pro. Both are overtaken:
  [0461](/adr/0461-the-pro-line-for-0-1-0-runtime-free-behind-entitlements-paid-studios-and-advanced-drivers) decision
  5 cancelled the Pro split for enterprise identity (batch stackra-191, STA-1343 cancelled) because identity over
  ZITADEL is runtime that OS identity uses, and made SSO a plan entitlement. The audit still read the older pages, so the
  ruling confirms the placement once, in one place.
- **What exists to build on.** The `IdpAdmin` port already creates and deactivates users, ends sessions and sets an
  organisation's `LoginPolicy`, including the exact `externalProviderIds` it offers
  ([0410](/adr/0410-identity-an-idp-admin-port-with-zitadel-and-memory-drivers-and-api-key-verification-by-hash)).
  The claims action stamps the Stackra user id (`usr_…`) into every token, so the principal is the Stackra id, not the
  provider's user. IAM has the `RoleGrants` port (grant only, no revoke, no provenance). API keys have a lifecycle
  (prefix, hash, rotation, expiry) that a provisioning token can reuse ([0278](/adr/0278-api-key-lifecycle)).
- **The plan ladder.** [0242](/adr/0242-commercial-model-plan-ladder-add-ons-metric-and-trial) sells SSO as a Pro
  add-on and in Business and Enterprise, and SCIM in Business and Enterprise; the 2026-11-08 amendments say
  "Enterprise-tier". The two disagree, and only entitlement data should decide it.

Alternatives weighed: a Stackra SAML or OIDC relying party of its own (rejected: 0206 keeps the login path out of our
services, and ZITADEL already brokers SAML and OIDC IdPs); ZITADEL's own SCIM server (weighed in decision 6); a
`@stackra-pro/identity-enterprise` package (rejected by 0461 decision 5 unless the ruling reverses it).

## Decision

Proposed. Each numbered rule is what the build will follow once accepted.

1. **Free code, gated by entitlements.** SSO and SCIM are built in `@stackra/identity` (contracts, memory and ZITADEL
   drivers, actions, migrations) with the IAM change of rule 9 in `@stackra/iam`. Nothing goes to `stackra-pro`. The
   runtime gate is the entitlement, never a package boundary: `access.sso` unlocks connections, domains, enforcement and
   group mapping from sign-in claims; `access.scim` unlocks the provisioning endpoint. A tenant without the entitlement
   gets "requires subscription" with the upgrade path (0242 decision 7,
   [0215](/adr/0215-entitlements)). Which tiers hold which key is plan data; the 0242 table is the default ladder, and
   "Enterprise-tier" in the 2026-11-08 amendments reads as "holds `access.sso`".
2. **One dedicated staff organisation per SSO tenant.** Turning SSO on for a tenant creates (once) a dedicated ZITADEL
   staff organisation for it on the instance of the tenant's region (0361 amendment, rule 3;
   [0353](/adr/0353-data-residency-a-region-fixed-at-sign-up-a-login-directory-that-only-routes-and-copies-within-a-jurisdiction)).
   Its users are owned by the enterprise: deactivating one ends that person's login everywhere, including at other
   merchants that invited them. The customer realm is out of scope (rule 13).
3. **Connections are ZITADEL organisation IdPs.** A connection is one SAML 2.0 or OIDC identity provider on the dedicated
   organisation, created through a new `IdpFederation` port in `@stackra/identity/contracts` (a separate port, so
   `IdpAdmin` does not keep growing), with a ZITADEL driver and a memory driver. The port adds, updates, lists and
   removes an organisation's SAML and OIDC providers, answers the organisation's SAML service-provider metadata and the
   OIDC redirect URL, and adds, verifies and removes organisation domains. Connections are created with automatic
   account creation and update on sign-in (just-in-time provisioning) and with account linking off. A tenant may hold
   more than one connection (an acquisition, a contractor IdP); each has its own domains.
4. **Secrets are write-only.** An OIDC client secret or a SAML signing key passes through the admin action to ZITADEL
   and is never stored by Stackra, returned to a client, logged or audited; the console shows "set on `<date>` by
   `<name>`". The IdP's public certificate and metadata URL are configuration, not secrets. Stackra records the
   certificate's expiry and emits `identity.connection.certificate_expiring` 30 days and 7 days before it.
5. **Verified domains and enforcement.** A domain joins a connection only after a DNS TXT proof on the dedicated
   organisation (ZITADEL's domain verification). The tenant setting `identity.sso.enforced` (an entitlement-gated
   setting value, `access.sso`, the shape the settings package already tests) makes the connection the only sign-in
   method for its verified domains: the dedicated organisation's `LoginPolicy` allows no password and no
   self-registration and lists exactly that connection's provider ids, and domain discovery on the login page sends
   an email of a verified domain to that organisation. `LoginPolicy` gains `allowDomainDiscovery` for this. Turning
   enforcement on is refused, `sso_enforcement_needs_break_glass`, unless at least one principal outside the dedicated
   organisation holds the tenant's owner role, so a broken IdP never locks a tenant out; the IAM break-glass grant
   (`BreakGlassGrant`) stays the emergency path for everyone else.
6. **SCIM 2.0 is served by Stackra, writing through the ports.** The identity service serves SCIM 2.0 (RFC 7643 and
   RFC 7644) at one base URL per region: `Users`, `Groups`, `ServiceProviderConfig`, `ResourceTypes` and `Schemas`.
   Users are written to ZITADEL through `IdpAdmin` (create, update, deactivate, remove); groups and memberships are kept
   by identity in its own tables, because the group-to-role mapping of rule 8 needs them and ZITADEL's own SCIM server,
   a preview feature, is documented for users, not groups (ruling 3 confirms this against the pinned version). The
   surface covers what Entra ID, Okta and Google Workspace send: filters
   `userName eq`, `externalId eq` and `displayName eq`; `PATCH` with `add`, `replace` and `remove`, including Entra's
   path-less and string-boolean forms; `startIndex` and `count` paging. It declares no bulk, sort, ETag or password
   change in `ServiceProviderConfig`. Errors use the RFC 7644 section 3.12 body. `externalId` is unique per
   connection, so a replayed create answers `409 uniqueness`.
7. **The SCIM token names the tenant.** Each connection has at most two live SCIM bearer tokens (rotation overlap),
   issued once, stored as hashes, prefixed and expiring as 0278 sets out for API keys. The token resolves the tenant and
   the connection; no path segment or header from the client chooses a tenant, and a token of tenant A finds nothing of
   tenant B. Calls are rate-limited per connection. Removing a connection revokes its tokens.
8. **Group-to-role mapping.** A mapping is one row: connection, external group (a SCIM group id, or the value of the
   `groups` claim or SAML attribute the connection names), role key and scope node inside the tenant's subtree. The
   identity service reconciles one person at a time: on each SCIM group change and, when the connection has no SCIM, on
   each sign-in from the assertion's groups. It adds the mapped grants the person lacks and revokes the mapped grants
   whose group they left. When a connection has SCIM, SCIM is the only source of its groups and assertion groups are
   ignored, so the two never fight. Grants made by hand are never touched. A mapping may name only a role its author
   could grant (0208 decision 4); it may never name the owner role or a role that holds a restricted permission, which
   stays a human grant by someone else (`Grant.grantedBy`). A person matched by no mapping signs in with no role and
   sees the "no access yet" state.
9. **`RoleGrants` gains provenance and revoke.** `Grant` gains `source` (`manual`, or `idp:<connection id>`) and
   `RoleGrants` gains `revoke(principal, role, scope, source)`, which removes only a grant of that source. The IAM
   service publishes a new policy snapshot after each change as for any grant (0208), so the effective-permission cache
   moves with it. This amends 0208 and is the only IAM change.
10. **Deprovisioning.** SCIM `active: false` or `DELETE`, or removing the person from every mapped group when the
    connection's setting says so, deactivates the provider user (`IdpAdmin.deactivateUser`), ends every session
    (`IdpAdmin.endSessions`) and revokes every grant whose source is that connection, in that order, in one audited
    action. `DELETE` removes the provider user after the tenant's retention setting; it never erases the person's data
    held by products, which follows the erasure path (STA-1543).
11. **Existing staff are claimed, never merged by name.** A person already in the shared staff organisation whose
    verified email is in a newly verified domain is moved by creating a user in the dedicated organisation that carries
    the same `usr_` id, then deactivating the old one after their first SSO sign-in. Their grants are on the `usr_` id
    and do not move. The tenant owner starts the claim per domain; each person is told before it happens. This follows
    0372 decision 6: the proof is the enterprise's verified domain plus the person's verified email, not a name.
12. **Risk, permissions and audit.** Identity declares `identity.federation.*` permissions above IAM
    ([0462](/adr/0462-iam-knows-no-segments-segments-is-an-add-on-and-one-layering-exemption-table)). Reading
    connections, domains and mappings is L1. Creating, changing or removing a connection, a domain, a mapping or
    enforcement, and issuing or rotating a SCIM token, are L4 with the `security` tag
    ([0099](/adr/0099-risk-scale-l0-to-l4)), need a step-up within 10 minutes (0207 decision 1,
    [0406](/adr/0406-step-up-is-an-action-option-checked-after-the-permission-and-the-call-carries-its-assurance)) and
    are never MCP tools. Every admin action, every SCIM write and every reconcile that changes a grant is audited
    ([0212](/adr/0212-audit-evidence-access-logs-hash-chain-and-retention)) with ids and counts: connection id, user
    `usr_` id, role key, scope, source. Never a group's member list, an assertion, a certificate or a secret.
13. **Out of scope.** SSO for a merchant's end customers (the customer realm) stays as 0361 sets it; national digital
    ID sign-in stays 0372; Stackra builds no login UI (0206 as amended by 0268). Each is a separate decision if asked
    for.

## Consequences

- An enterprise tenant connects its IdP, verifies its domains, maps groups to roles and turns on SCIM from the console,
  with no Stackra release and no Figentra operator in the loop.
- `@stackra/identity` grows a second admin port, a SCIM protocol surface and four tables (connections, domains, group
  mappings and memberships, SCIM tokens). Each table is tenant-owned with row-level security and ships the tenancy
  isolation test.
- `@stackra/iam` changes `Grant` and `RoleGrants` (rule 9); every `RoleGrants` driver and the IAM service follow.
- SCIM user attributes are personal data, classified `pii` under
  [0134](/adr/0134-data-invariants-classification-and-delete-lifecycle). Stackra stores `userName`, name, primary email,
  `active` and `externalId` only; enterprise-extension attributes (department, manager, employee number) are dropped
  unless the ruling says otherwise. Group display names are tenant data. Identity's `AGENTS.md` PII table gains these
  rows in the build.
- Losing `access.sso` or `access.scim` freezes, never breaks: existing connections keep signing people in, edits are
  refused, and SCIM answers `403` with the RFC 7644 error body, so a lapsed plan cannot lock staff out.
- Build rows, filed when accepted (size XL in all): `IdpFederation` port with memory and ZITADEL drivers, and
  `LoginPolicy.allowDomainDiscovery`; connection, domain and enforcement actions with migrations; `Grant.source` and
  `RoleGrants.revoke` in IAM; the reconcile service; the SCIM 2.0 server; the account claim; console screens in OS;
  docs and a runbook for each IdP vendor.

## Rulings asked for

Each question has the recommended answer first. The CTO rules; nothing here is accepted until then.

1. **Core or Pro.** Recommended: free code in `@stackra/identity` behind `access.sso` and `access.scim` (rule 1), as
   0461 decision 5 already says. The alternative is a `@stackra-pro` package, which would need 0461 amended.
2. **Which tiers.** Recommended: entitlement data decides, with 0242's ladder as the default (SSO a Pro add-on and in
   Business and Enterprise; SCIM in Business and Enterprise), and the "Enterprise-tier" wording of the 2026-11-08
   amendments read as "holds `access.sso`". The alternative is Enterprise only.
3. **Who serves SCIM.** Recommended: Stackra's identity service (rule 6), because group-to-role mapping needs groups. The
   alternative, ZITADEL's own SCIM server, is less code but leaves groups to the sign-in claims only and is a preview
   feature; confirm its group support against the pinned ZITADEL version before choosing it.
4. **Claiming existing staff by verified domain** (rule 11). Recommended: yes, started by the tenant owner, each person
   told first. The alternative is that existing staff keep their shared-organisation login until they leave.
5. **Session length under enforced SSO.** Recommended: the 0207 baseline of 30 days stays, and SCIM deactivation ends
   sessions at once; a tenant without SCIM may set a shorter maximum (as low as 12 hours) so a person removed at the IdP
   loses access within a day.
6. **Enterprise-extension attributes.** Recommended: dropped (data minimisation); a product that needs one asks for it
   in its own ADR.

## Enforcement

planned — the build adds: a conformance suite run against the memory driver and against a ZITADEL container in CI for
`IdpFederation` (0206 Enforcement); the tenancy isolation test for each new table and for the SCIM surface (a token of
tenant A reads, writes and lists nothing of tenant B); a test that a mapping naming the owner role or a role with a
restricted permission is refused; a test that reconcile never revokes a `manual` grant; a test that enforcement without
an outside owner is refused; the Microsoft Entra SCIM validator and Okta's SCIM test suite run against a staging
deployment before the feature is offered; gen's `step_up_missing` (`checkCallShape`) already refuses an L4 user action
without `stepUp`. Nothing checks this page today.
