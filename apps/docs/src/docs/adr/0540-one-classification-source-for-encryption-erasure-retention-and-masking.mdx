## Context

A field's handling was stated in up to four places that nothing compared: `@Classified(DataClassification.X)` of
`@stackra/validation` (export, erasure, the logs), `@Classify(DataClass)` of `@stackra/model` with four access effects
(AI, support, search, export), `HasEncryptedFields` naming the sealed fields, and a `PersonalData({ fields })`
declaration of `@stackra/privacy` naming each erased field and its method. A health field could rest in clear, a
`PersonalData` declaration could anonymise a field its product had decided to shred, and gen could not see either,
because it did not read `PersonalData` and no class said how its fields are kept. ADR 0134 decision 2 promised "fixed
effects" per class that were never listed for encryption, erasure, retention or masking.

## Decision

1. **Eight effects.** A data class sets eight effects, all required: the four access effects of 0341 (`ai`, `support`,
   `search`, `export`) and four keeping effects:
   - `encrypt`: `clear`, `sealed` (named by `HasEncryptedFields`) or `subject_sealed` (named by
     `HasEncryptedFields({ subject })`, so erasing the subject shreds it);
   - `erasure`: `keep` (not the subject's personal data), `anonymise`, `delete` or `shred`;
   - `retention`: `none`, or `required` (a model holding the field declares `Retention`);
   - `mask`: what a masked copy writes, `keep`, `hash` (keyed, joins still match), `fake` or `null`.

   `DataClass({...})` refuses a class that leaves an effect out or names an unknown code.

2. **The built-in classifications are data classes, in one table.** `@Classified(DataClassification.X)` means the
   built-in class `X`, whose effects are row `X` of `DATA_CLASSIFICATION_EFFECTS` in `@stackra/contracts`. That table is
   the one statement; `@stackra/model` (`builtInDataClass`), `@stackra/privacy` and gen read it, and
   `CredentialDataClass` is held equal to the `credential` row by a test.

   | Class          | `ai`            | `support` | `search`     | `export`   | `encrypt` | `erasure`   | `retention` | `mask` |
   | -------------- | --------------- | --------- | ------------ | ---------- | --------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------ |
   | `personal`     | `allowed`       | `visible` | `snippet`    | `labelled` | `clear`   | `anonymise` | `none`      | `fake` |
   | `financial`    | `allowed`       | `visible` | `snippet`    | `labelled` | `clear`   | `anonymise` | `none`      | `fake` |
   | `health`       | `tenant_opt_in` | `hidden`  | `no_snippet` | `labelled` | `sealed`  | `anonymise` | `required`  | `null` |
   | `pseudonymous` | `allowed`       | `visible` | `snippet`    | `labelled` | `clear`   | `anonymise` | `none`      | `hash` |
   | `regulated`    | `tenant_opt_in` | `hidden`  | `no_snippet` | `labelled` | `clear`   | `anonymise` | `none`      | `fake` |
   | `credential`   | `never`         | `hidden`  | `no_snippet` | `excluded` | `sealed`  | `keep`      | `none`      | `null` |
   | `privileged`   | `tenant_opt_in` | `hidden`  | `no_snippet` | `labelled` | `clear`   | `keep`      | `none`      | `null` |

   `privileged` is `PrivilegedDataClass` of `@stackra/model` (0341 decision 3); it is not a `DataClassification` code.
   `regulated` stays `clear` so the products already storing regulated identifiers are not forced into a re-seal by an
   upgrade; a product that must seal them declares a stricter class.

3. **The floor is an order, in code.** `DATA_CLASS_EFFECT_RANKS` ranks each effect's codes from loosest to strictest
   (0380 decision 3, extended): `allowed` < `tenant_opt_in` < `never`; `visible` < `hidden`; `snippet` < `no_snippet`;
   `labelled` < `excluded`; `clear` < `sealed` < `subject_sealed`; `keep` < `anonymise` < `delete` = `shred`; `none` <
   `required`; `keep` < `hash` < `fake` < `null`. A class never loosens its floor (`loosenedEffects`).

4. **One effective class per field.** A field's class is its `@Classify` class, its `@Classified` built-in class, or,
   when it carries both, the stricter code of the two on every effect under the `@Classify` key (`strictestEffects`).
   `fieldDataClasses` of `@stackra/model` and gen's `fieldDataClassOf` apply the same rule; gen refuses a `@Classify`
   class looser than the field's `@Classified` class, so the merge only guards a member gen has not checked.

5. **`PersonalData` is derived.** A `PersonalData({ model, subject })` declaration may leave `fields` out: its fields are
   then the model's classified fields whose class's `erasure` is not `keep`, each with that method. A listed field list
   stays authoritative (a personal field on the row may belong to another person), but no listed method may be weaker
   than its field's class, and a credential is never listed (0494 rule 4). The erasure workflow refuses a weaker method
   (`looserErasureFields`), and gen refuses it at build time. Erasure fails closed: a declaration that ends with no
   field (it lists none, or an empty list, and no class of its model derives one) would erase nothing and still let the
   receipt report the subject erased, so gen refuses it (`personal_data_no_fields`) and the erasure workflow refuses to
   start while one is registered (`emptyPersonalDataDeclarations`).

6. **Who reads each keeping effect.** Gen reads `encrypt` (the field must be named by `HasEncryptedFields`, with
   `subject` for `subject_sealed`), `retention` (the model declares `Retention`) and `erasure` (each `PersonalData`
   declaration of an own model). `@stackra/privacy` reads `erasure` to derive declarations. `maskOf` of `@stackra/model`
   gives each field's `mask`, which the masked-copy job (data-layer gap gov15) applies; no masker keeps its own list of
   columns. An encrypted field still carries `@Classified`: the log redaction (`redact` of `@stackra/logger`), the
   panel export and `changeCaptureOf` read only `@Classified`, so gen's `encrypted_field_unclassified` does not count a
   `@Classify` class alone (a sealed field with only `@Classify` would be logged and exported in clear once decrypted).
   Moving those readers onto the table is a later change; until then `@Classify` adds to `@Classified`, never replaces
   it, on a field that holds personal or secret data.

7. **Built-in classes are recognised by package.** A published package carries a class's declaration (`.d.ts`), not the
   `DataClass({...})` call gen reads a product class's effects from, so gen could not see the effects of
   `@Classify(CredentialDataClass)` in a product. Gen resolves the `@Classify` argument through the type checker to its
   class declaration (an alias or a re-export resolves to the original) and, when that declaration belongs to the
   `@stackra/model` package and its name is a key of `BUILT_IN_DATA_CLASSES` in `@stackra/contracts`, takes the class's
   key and effects from that table. The package is read from the declaration's own `package.json`, never from the import
   text. Recognising by a symbol (a brand property on the class) was the alternative: it is rejected because a product
   class could carry the same brand and claim a built-in's effects, and a brand is a type-level value gen would have to
   read out of a `.d.ts`, while a declaration's package is fixed by where it is installed. A same-named class in any
   other package is a product class, read from its own `DataClass({...})` call or, when gen cannot read it, counted
   only through its `@Classified` code. `@stackra/model`'s tests hold each built-in class equal to its row.

## Consequences

- A field is classified once. Its encryption, erasure, retention and masking follow from its class, and gen names the
  first place a model or a declaration disagrees with it.
- A product data class must state all eight effects; a class declared before this change fails to type-check and is
  refused by `DataClass` until it states the four keeping effects.
- A model holding a `health` field must encrypt it and declare `Retention`; gen refuses it otherwise.
- `@stackra/contracts` carries the classification table, so a package that cannot import `@stackra/model` (gen today,
  the logger once its redaction moves onto the table) reads the same effects.
- The seeded `data-class` records of 0380 decision 1 remain a later change: today the table is code, and a tenant's
  tightening through settings is not built.

## Enforcement

- `@stackra/model` `DataClass` refuses a missing or unknown effect (`src/core/bases/__tests__/data-class.base.test.ts`);
  `CredentialDataClass` equals the table's `credential` row (same test).
- `@stackra/contracts` fixes the table and the ranks
  (`src/core/constants/__tests__/data-classification-effects.constant.test.ts`,
  `src/core/functions/__tests__/loosened-effects.function.test.ts`).
- `@stackra/gen`, full pass: `checkEncryptedFields` (`classified_field_not_encrypted`,
  `classified_field_not_subject_sealed`, `credential_field_not_encrypted`, `encrypted_field_unclassified`),
  `checkDataClasses` (`data_class_effects_missing`, `data_class_looser_than_classified`,
  `classified_model_without_retention`) and `checkPersonalData` (`personal_data_field_unknown`,
  `personal_data_credential_field`, `personal_data_looser_than_class`, `personal_data_shred_unsealed`,
  `personal_data_anonymise_not_nullable`, `personal_data_no_fields`); the source reader takes a built-in class's
  effects from `BUILT_IN_DATA_CLASSES` by package (`src/node/functions/__tests__/create-source-reader.function.test.ts`).
- `@stackra/privacy` `EraseSubjectWorkflow` refuses a declared method weaker than its field's class before any row is
  touched, and refuses to start while a declaration resolves to no field
  (`src/node/workflows/__tests__/erase-subject.workflow.test.ts`).
- `@stackra/contracts` fixes the built-in classes' rows (`src/core/constants/__tests__/built-in-data-classes.constant.test.ts`);
  `@stackra/model` holds `CredentialDataClass` and `PrivilegedDataClass` equal to them.
- planned: the masked-copy job applying `maskOf` (gap gov15) and the seeded `data-class` records of 0380.
