## Context

Commerce has the shopper's model (carts, checkout, orders, returns, tenders) and its actions (`SetCartLineAction`,
`CheckoutCartAction`, ...). Shoppers will meet the product in the storefront, in WhatsApp and inside assistant hosts
(ChatGPT, Claude) through the MCP gateway. Each surface would otherwise grow its own cards and its own checkout. 0360
decision 8 says no mode, setting or session skips a named human for money movement; it was written for staff (refunds,
payouts, price changes) and does not say who the named human is when a shopper pays for their own order. Read
literally it would make an agent-assisted checkout impossible, or push a staff member into every sale.

## Decision

1. **The shopping assistant is an ordinary agent.** `ShopperAgent` (an `Agent` of `@stackra/ai`) lives in Commerce's
   storefront module. Its tools are Commerce queries and shopper actions, generated as every tool is: catalogue
   search, filter, sort and facets (`@stackra/search` under record visibility, 0492), product, availability and price
   for the shopper's market, cart actions, the shopper's orders, tracking, returns. Its actor is the shopper: a
   signed-in customer, or a guest session that may browse and fill a cart but must sign in or verify a contact (OTP)
   before checkout. No staff permission is ever reachable from a shopper session.

2. **Cards are shared.** Commerce declares `ProductCard`, `ProductListCard`, `CartCard`, `OrderCard`,
   `OrderTrackingCard` and `CheckoutCard` as `Card`s (0490 decision 14). The storefront renders the web and native
   components; MCP hosts receive the `ui://commerce/<card>` bundle; WhatsApp receives the message projection (an
   interactive message with an image and up to three reply buttons, or a product list message). One card, one
   output schema, three renderers.

3. **Tracking comes from events.** Commerce keeps an `order_timeline` projection fed by its order, payment and
   fulfilment integration events; `orders.order.track` reads it. Proactive updates (shipped, out for delivery,
   delivered, delayed) go through the notifications service on the channel the shopper consented to, WhatsApp
   included, under the consent rules of 0472.

4. **B2B.** A company buyer acts under the company's account and price lists; a quote, a PO number and payment terms
   are inputs of the same checkout. An order above the buyer's limit goes to the company's approver by Escalate
   (0360 decision 9): the approver takes the order over, the buyer's agent never borrows the approver's authority.
   Payment on terms moves no money at checkout; the invoice settles it later under the staff rules.

5. **Marketplace.** A multi-seller cart splits at checkout into one order per seller with one payment that the
   connection mode of 0390 divides. Each seller's staff work their orders through the staff sidekick under the staff
   floor. A shopper's question about a seller's order is answered from that order's timeline; a seller never sees
   another seller's lines.

6. **The payer is the named human for their own payment (clarifies 0360 decision 8 item 1).** For an action tagged
   `financial` whose only money movement is the actor paying for their own order from their own payment method, the
   named human of decision 8 is that actor, and their approval is a payer step-up that the agent cannot perform:
   - a wallet sheet (Apple Pay, Google Pay) confirmed on the device with its own biometric or passcode;
   - a card authentication (3-D Secure) challenge the issuer presents;
   - a one-time code sent to the shopper's verified phone or email and typed by the shopper, where the method has
     neither of the above (cash on delivery, payment on terms, a stored method).
     The agent may build the cart and call `commerce.checkout.prepare`, which returns a payment intent that needs the
     payer's confirmation and binds the cart version, the total, the currency and the payee. Confirmation happens only
     in a payer surface: the wallet sheet or 3-D Secure frame in the storefront and the apps, or, on WhatsApp and in an
     MCP host, a `CheckoutCard` whose button opens a short-lived hosted payment page on the storefront bound to that
     intent. Any change to the cart voids the intent. The agent never holds a card number, never confirms a payment
     intent, and never sees a one-time code.

7. **Staff money stays on the existing floor.** Refunds, voids, store credit, discounts, price and coupon activation,
   payouts and every other `financial` action by a staff actor keep a named staff human on every call, whatever
   the mode. Applying a coupon code the shopper typed to their own cart is not activation and is not `financial`; an
   agent offering a discount that no rule grants is refused.

8. **Day one, not later (amends 0348 decision 7).** MCP host rendering of cards and WhatsApp chat (the WhatsApp
   Business Platform's messages and interactive messages through the OS integrations service) are day-one surfaces.
   WhatsApp Flows stay tier later. Meta business verification and each host's directory review stay owner steps.

## Consequences

- Commerce owns the shopper agent, its skills and its cards; Stackra owns the `Card` kind, the renderers, the
  `ui://` build and the WhatsApp projection.
- `commerce.checkout.prepare` and a payer-confirmation webhook path are Commerce work; `CheckoutCartAction` becomes
  the server half that runs only after confirmation.
- The approval gate of `@stackra/ai` gains a `payer` approval kind: satisfied by the payment provider's confirmed
  event for the bound intent, or by a verified one-time code, never by a tool call.
- An abandoned agent-built cart is an ordinary abandoned cart.

## Enforcement

- planned — STA-1635: the approval gate test that a `financial` action whose actor is staff never resolves through a
  `payer` approval, and that a `payer` approval for one intent never confirms another.
- planned — STA-1635: the runtime test that no tool output, log or stored message carries a card number or a one-time
  code (the guardrail redaction rules of `@stackra/ai`).
- planned — Commerce: the checkout test that a cart change after `prepare` voids the intent.
