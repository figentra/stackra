## Context

A `CheckoutRequest` carries a tax-inclusive total, an optional tip and optional lines. Tamara asks for `tax_amount` and
`shipping_amount` on every order, so its driver sends zero for both, which misstates the order to the provider and to
its credit scoring. Other buy-now-pay-later and invoice providers ask for the same split. Separately,
`PaymentService.refund` moves the payment to `partially_refunded` or `refunded` as soon as the provider accepts a refund,
even when the provider answers `pending`; a pending refund that later fails leaves the record wrong, and the next refund
is checked against an amount that was never returned. Alternatives weighed: computing tax in payments (rejected: tax is
the pricing and tax ports' answer, and payments must never hold a rate) and refusing pending refunds (rejected: several
providers only ever answer pending first).

## Decision

1. `CheckoutRequest` gains two optional fields, `tax` and `shipping`, each a `Money` in the request's currency and each
   already included in `amount`. The product fills them from the order it priced (pricing and tax ports); payments
   never computes them. A driver whose provider takes the split sends them; a driver whose provider does not ignores
   them. When they are absent, a provider that requires them gets zero, as today, and the driver says so in its file.
2. A refund is counted against the payment only once it is confirmed. `PaymentRecord` keeps `refunded` (confirmed) and
   gains `refunding` (accepted, not yet confirmed), each keyed by the refund reference. A refund is refused when
   `amount` exceeds `total - refunded - refunding`. A `pending` answer adds to `refunding`; a `succeeded` answer, a later
   lookup through `reconcile`, or a verified provider webhook moves it to `refunded`; a `failed` one removes it. The
   payment's status moves to `partially_refunded` or `refunded` only from confirmed amounts.
3. Neither change adds a Market, rate or provider to code: which providers need the split is each driver's mapping of
   its own API, and when a refund counts is mechanism.

## Consequences

Tamara and similar providers receive true order data. Refund state can no longer run ahead of the provider, and two
concurrent refunds cannot together exceed the total. Products must pass `tax` and `shipping` where they have them, and the
payments service's durable `PaymentRecords` driver gains the `refunding` column. Reconciliation of pending refunds now
matters: a host that never runs `reconcile` or receives webhooks leaves refunds pending.

## Enforcement

planned — `payment.service.test.ts` cases for a pending refund that later succeeds and one that fails, and a Tamara
driver test that sends the given `tax` and `shipping`.
