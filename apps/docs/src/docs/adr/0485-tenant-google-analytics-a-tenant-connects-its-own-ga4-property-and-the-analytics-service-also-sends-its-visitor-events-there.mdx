## Context

[0483](/adr/0483-analytics-relay-apps-send-product-analytics-only-to-the-analytics-service) routes every app's
analytics through the `relay` driver to the product's analytics service, which fans out server-side to the
deployment's own PostHog and GA4. That is Figentra measuring its own product, with Figentra as the controller; 0483
decision 5 left a tenant's analytics in its own Google Analytics to a separate decision. The owner wants a business on
the platform to see its own visitors (shoppers on its storefront, parents on its school site, users of its branded app)
in its own GA4 property, as it would with the Google tag on a site it built itself. The parts exist: a tenant's
credentials live in the connection store as a tenant `Connection` with `@Secret` fields
([0107](/adr/0107-connections-store-and-three-key-homes), [0308](/adr/0308-integrations-v3-1-provider-descriptions-connection-stores-and-webhooks)
rules 2, 3, 12), a setting points at a credential through `@ConnectionRef` ([0108](/adr/0108-scoped-settings) S4), the
ingest action knows the verified consent snapshot ([0472](/adr/0472-server-side-consent-a-signed-short-lived-consent-snapshot-travels-with-server-calls)),
and the `ga4` Measurement Protocol driver is planned in `@stackra-pro/analytics-destinations`
([0461](/adr/0461-the-pro-line-for-0-1-0-runtime-free-behind-entitlements-paid-studios-and-advanced-drivers) decision 2).
Alternatives weighed: the Google tag on the tenant's pages through the browser `AdPixel` port (rejected: it loads a
vendor script and cookies on the visitor's device, which 0483 decision 1 removed), and a periodic export from the
warehouse to the tenant's BigQuery (rejected for this need: it is business data, not visitor behaviour, and is covered
by the `WarehouseSink` of [0163](/adr/0163-reports-exports-imports-and-declared-read-models)).

## Decision

1. **The tenant is the controller, we are its processor.** Events of a tenant's visitors that we send to the
   tenant's GA4 property are the tenant's personal data processed on its instruction. Connecting the property is that
   instruction, recorded as an audited action with the actor and time. The data processing agreement
   ([0189](/adr/0189-privacy-operations-and-data-retention) decision 5) gains a clause: on the controller's
   instruction, the processor forwards visitor analytics events to a Google Analytics property the controller owns;
   Google processes them under the controller's own terms with Google, and is not our sub-processor for that flow.
   The record of processing lists the flow. Our own GA4 of 0483 stays on the sub-processor list as before.
2. **The property is a tenant connection.** A `Ga4Integration` (provider `google-analytics-4`, levels
   `[ConnectionLevel.Tenant]`, `AuthType.None`) and a `Ga4Connection` with two fields ship beside the `ga4` driver in
   `@stackra-pro/analytics-destinations`: `measurementId` (plain, `G-` and ten characters, shown in the console) and
   `apiSecret` (`@Secret("GA4_API_SECRET")`). The secret is a tenant secret: held in the connection store, encrypted
   under the tenant's data key with the tenant, connection and field bound as additional data (0308 rule 3,
   [0188](/adr/0188-operator-access-and-the-encryption-key-hierarchy)).
3. **The secret is written blind and never read back.** The console form and the API take `apiSecret` on create and
   on replace only; no read, list, export, log, audit entry or error carries it, and the console shows only that it is
   set and when. Only the fan-out listener resolves it, through the runtime at send time (0107 decision 4); an agent,
   an operator view and a support export see the `@ConnectionRef`. Disconnecting destroys the stored value (0308 rule
   7: GA4 has no revocation endpoint). `check()` posts one validation event to the Measurement Protocol's debug
   endpoint and fails on a malformed id; it cannot prove the secret, because GA4 accepts a wrong one silently, so the
   console says so and points the tenant to its realtime report.
4. **A setting turns it on.** The analytics service owns the setting `analytics.scopeGa4`, set at the `TenancyScopes.Tenant` level
   ([0108](/adr/0108-scoped-settings) S1, S2): `connection` (`@ConnectionRef<Ga4Connection>`), `category` (the consent
   category that gates it, default `analytics`) and `enabled`. A deployment may lock it off per region or per plan
   (0108 S2). No setting holds the id or the secret.
5. **Which events: the request's tenant, its visitor surfaces only.** The ingest action
   (`IngestAnalyticsBatchAction`) runs in the tenant context the host resolves from the request (the tenant's
   storefront, site or branded-app API host); a tenant or scope id in the envelope, such as `groups.scope`, is never
   trusted for routing. When that tenant's `analytics.scopeGa4` is enabled and the verified snapshot grants its
   category, the action publishes one more `analytics.batch.accepted.v1` for the destination `scope-ga4`, carrying the
   request's `ScopeRef` (the tenant) from context and the deliveries that destination takes. It takes only events the product declares as
   visitor events: a surface owned by the tenant (storefront, site, branded app, portal) marks its events tenant
   visible, and the tenant staff's use of Figentra's own console and apps never is. `identify` and `group` deliveries
   are not sent. Events with no tenant context (Figentra's marketing site, sign-up) never reach a tenant destination.
6. **The fan-out sends with the tenant's credentials.** `FanOutAnalyticsListener` handles a `scope-ga4` event in
   that tenant's context: it resolves the connection from the setting, and the `ga4` driver sends with that
   connection's `measurementId` and `apiSecret` instead of the deployment's. Each tenant's sends retry alone through
   the outbox, as each destination does in 0483 decision 4; a missing, disabled or disconnected connection drops the
   event and logs `analytics.scope_destination.skipped` with the tenant and reason, and never retries. What GA4
   receives is what 0483 decisions 3 and 6 allow and no more: the envelope's event name and declared properties,
   `client_id` set to the anonymous id, the session id, never `user_id`, an IP address, a user agent or an advertising
   id.
7. **Consent is the visitor's, decided once, gated per destination.** The visitor's snapshot gates the tenant
   destination by its own category, independently of the deployment's destinations, so a visitor who grants
   `analytics` but whose tenant chose `marketing` reaches our PostHog and not the tenant's GA4. The consent banner
   shown on the tenant's surface must name Google Analytics as a recipient while the tenant destination is enabled;
   enabling or disabling it changes that surface's consent policy version, so visitors are asked again (0298
   decision 3).
8. **Every region.** The tenant destination is available in every region's stack and is sent from the region that
   holds the tenant ([0353](/adr/0353-data-residency-a-region-fixed-at-sign-up-a-login-directory-that-only-routes-and-copies-within-a-jurisdiction)
   decision 5). The transfer to Google is the controller's choice of its own vendor, which the console states before
   it connects; the events carry no direct identifier ([0071](/adr/0071-privacy-obligations) decision 6). A deployment
   that must not send a region's data out of its jurisdiction locks the setting off there (decision 4).
9. **Code tier and plan.** The per-tenant destination mechanism (the setting kind, the tenant fan-out branch of the
   ingest action and listener, the tenant-visible event marker) is runtime and ships free in `@stackra/analytics/node`
   (0461 decision 1). Its identifiers speak of a scope through `ScopeRef`, never of a tenant
   ([0323](/adr/0323-tenant-vocabulary-lives-only-in-tenancy-and-scopes-use-the-scoperef-port)); the tenant is
   Figentra's name for the root scope. The `ga4` driver, `Ga4Integration` and `Ga4Connection` are Pro, in
   `@stackra-pro/analytics-destinations` (0461 decision 2); Figentra uses them under its internal licence. Which of
   Figentra's plans include it is an entitlement ([0242](/adr/0242-commercial-model-plan-ladder-add-ons-metric-and-trial)),
   checked when the setting is enabled and at each fan-out.
10. **Erasure and retention.** Our hop keeps nothing: an `analytics.batch.accepted.v1` row lives only until it is
    sent or dead-lettered, under the outbox's retention. GA4 receives no `user_id`, so nothing there is keyed to a
    person we can name, and the Measurement Protocol cannot delete; a data-subject request about what the tenant's
    property holds is the controller's to run in its own GA4, and our erasure receipt
    ([0189](/adr/0189-privacy-operations-and-data-retention) decision 2) says so. Withdrawal stops new sends at the
    visitor's next snapshot (0472 consequence 3); disconnecting stops them at once. GA4's data retention is the
    tenant's property setting; the console recommends the shortest. On offboarding (0189 decision 3) the connection
    and the setting are deleted with the tenant.
11. **Not the built-in dashboards.** analytics-reports' tenant dashboards and the warehouse
    ([0190](/adr/0190-seed-profiles-and-product-analytics) decision 3, [0163](/adr/0163-reports-exports-imports-and-declared-read-models)
    note of 2026-10-29) read the tenant's business data (orders, bookings, enrolments) from read models and the
    `WarehouseSink`, stay in the tenant's region and are served by us. The tenant GA4 destination carries only visitor
    behaviour events, leaves the platform for the tenant's own Google property and is read in Google's tools. Neither
    replaces the other, and no business record or personal field flows to GA4.

## Consequences

- A tenant sees its visitors in its own GA4 with no script, cookie or SDK on its pages and the same consent decision
  that gates our own analytics.
- The analytics service gains a per-tenant fan-out: one outbox event per enabled tenant per batch, and a connection
  lookup per send; it must cache the resolved connection briefly and drop the cache on `settings.changed.v1`.
- GA4 reports that depend on the Google tag (engagement time, demographics, Google signals, ads linking) stay partial
  for tenants, as they are for us (0483 consequences); the console says so before it connects.
- A wrong API secret is invisible to us: events simply do not appear in the tenant's property.
- Measurement Protocol has no dedup, so an outbox retry can count an event twice in the tenant's property.
- The DPA, the record of processing and the consent banner text for tenant surfaces need a change before release;
  legal review is the product's ([0071](/adr/0071-privacy-obligations) decision 5).

## Enforcement

planned — `@stackra/analytics/node` tests: a tenant destination fires only in the request's tenant context and never
from an envelope id; only tenant-visible events and only when the snapshot grants the setting's category; `identify`
and `group` are not sent; a missing or disabled connection is skipped without retry; `user_id` never reaches the
driver. `@stackra-pro/analytics-destinations` tests: `apiSecret` is absent from every read model, log and error, and
the driver uses the connection's credentials. The tenancy isolation test: tenant B's batch never reaches tenant A's
property. Linear: STA-1628.
