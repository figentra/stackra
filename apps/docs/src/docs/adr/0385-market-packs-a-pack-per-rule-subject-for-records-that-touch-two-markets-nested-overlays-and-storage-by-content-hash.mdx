## Context

0370 resolves one market per tenant. US law stacks federal, state and city rules (US-3: `us` → `us-ca` → `us-ca-sf`),
and one sale can owe two markets: a New York shop selling to a California buyer owes California's sales-tax and
privacy rules on that sale while its tips and labour rules stay New York's (US-4). The review (R3-SO-01, X2-18) found
three gaps: overlays deeper than two levels were not confirmed, a product section could not sit on an overlay (the
restaurant's `us-ca-sf` rules, Juristra's DIFC defaults), and `MarketContext` gave one pack per record. 0363 asked
whether the strictest pack or the legal entity's pack wins. Alternatives weighed: the strictest value across both
markets (rejected: "strictest" has no meaning for a tax rate or a provider, and it applies a law that does not govern
the sale), and always the entity's pack (rejected: it gives the buyer's state no say over its own sales tax and
privacy law). 0370 also left where pack bytes are stored to the caller, so every service would invent its own fetch.

## Decision

1. **No pack wins for a tenant; each rule kind names its subject.** A pack carries `subjects`: a key or key prefix
   mapped to `location` (where the work or sale happens), `buyer` (the ship-to or billing address), `entity` (where
   the legal entity is formed) or `tenant` (the tenant's own market). The longest matching prefix wins; an overlay may
   restate one. With no match the tenant's market answers, as before. The subjects are pack data; no rule kind is
   named in code.
2. **`MarketContext.packFor(record, ruleKind)`.** A `MarketContext` is built per tenant from the verified packs and the
   tenant's resolve options. `packFor` reads the rule kind's subject from the tenant's resolved market and returns the
   resolved market of the record's market for that subject (`MarketRecord`: `tenant`, `location`, `buyer`, `entity`).
   A record without the subject's market is refused (`subject_market_missing`); the caller supplies it, never a
   guess. Rules facts carry one resolved pack per subject.
3. **Floors follow the subject.** A legal floor is enforced from the pack the rule kind's subject resolves to, and
   only from it. Nothing merges floors across markets; a tenant who wants a stricter value sets it above the floor in
   its settings.
4. **Overlays of any depth.** `extends` chains of any length resolve parent first, and a loop of any length is refused
   (`overlay_cycle`). 0370 decision 3 is read this way.
5. **Product sections per overlay.** A section is declared per market in the chain (`us-ca-sf` / `restaurant`); each
   resolves beside its own base pack, so an overlay's section overlays its parent's section. A section still has no
   `extends` of its own and its keys keep its prefix (0370 decision 7).
6. **Storage and fetch by content hash.** Signed packs are stored and fetched through the `PackStore` port, addressed
   by their content hash. The production driver keeps one file per pack at `market-packs/<hash>.json` on an injected
   `FileSystem` disk (`@stackra/filesystem`), refuses a pack whose hash does not match, and never rewrites a stored
   hash. `fetchPacks(store, hashes, trustedKeys)` loads the hashes a deployment names and verifies each; a hash the
   store lacks is refused (`not_stored`). Packs a module ships as `data/` assets are read through `AssetLibrary`
   (`@stackra/assets`) after the manifest hash check. Which hashes a deployment loads and its trusted keys are
   deployment configuration.
7. **Seed packs ship as draft data.** The `us` base pack and the overlays of the first launch list (`us-ny`, `us-nj`,
   `us-ca`, `us-tx`, `us-il`, `us-mi`, `us-fl`, US-2) ship as `draft` data assets of `@stackra/market-packs`, with a
   `counsel_review` checklist item and no sign-offs. They carry the subjects of US-4, the federal and state holiday
   sets and the first calendar values (0387). Draft is never served outside authoring (0370 decision 4).
8. **Bindings.** `@stackra/fiscal` answers `FiscalPackSource` from packs (`fiscal.invoice`, `fiscal.till`). The
   payments binding (`MarketPaymentDefaults`) follows in its own package.

## Consequences

- A cross-state or cross-border sale reads each rule from the market that governs it; the subject table is reviewed
  data, so counsel can change it without a release.
- Every caller of `packFor` must fill the record's markets from its addresses; a missing buyer address on a taxable
  sale now fails loudly.
- Pack bytes have one home and one address; a tampered or misfiled pack stops start-up.
- `@stackra/market-packs` now depends on `@stackra/assets`, `@stackra/container` and `@stackra/filesystem`, and ships
  data assets (drafts only; base packs remain owned by the platform).
