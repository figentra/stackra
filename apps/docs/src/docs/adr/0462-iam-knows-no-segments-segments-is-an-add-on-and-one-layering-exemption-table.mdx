## Context

`@stackra/segments` declares one action, `segments.segment.recompute@1` (`RecomputeSegmentsAction`), the hourly
membership recompute. Its only actor is `ActorKind.Job`. gen refused it with `permission_unknown`, because no
`Permissions` class declared `segments.segment.recompute`. Segments could not declare one: the `Permissions` base lives
in `@stackra/iam`, and `@stackra/iam` depended on `@stackra/segments` for one type, `SegmentRef`, in its `Audience`
union (`ScopeRef | SegmentRef | RoleRef`). The import would have closed a package cycle, which turbo refuses
(BUILDER-RULES rule 6c). The owner asked on 2026-10-27 to settle this in the ADRs and to find every other case like it.

The stackra-210 audit (`notes/handover/batches/permission-and-exemption-audit.md`) read core at `4a12b67`, OS `main`
at `72f073d8` and Commerce `commerce/wave-a` at `543d625`:

- Of 689 actions, 2 declare no permission. One is RecomputeSegments, the only one where a cycle is the cause. The other
  is OS `traffic.hit.record`, a public anonymous counter whose authors chose no permission; it is not a job.
- 66 actions have only Job or System actors. 65 declare a permission: 25 system-held (`Permission.Lx.system()`, "held
  only by the platform; no role may grant it") and 40 grantable.
- The iam-to-segments edge was that one type import.
- The same layering shape, a low package that cannot use a kind base above it, occurs in 9 more classes and one
  generated file: 7 `@stackra/contracts` drivers that cannot call `Driver(port)`, the `@stackra/config` and
  `@stackra/container` errors that cannot extend `Refusal`, and the `@stackra/testing` test setup. Lint accepts most of
  these through path exemptions in `stackra/base-matches-suffix`; gen refuses all of them. The audit finds 8 classes
  of exemption where the two disagree (STA-1365 items 1 to 4, 8 and 9).

The owner's decision of 2026-10-28, in their words: "segment is a business level, not a permission level", and "I want
a segment to be like ... an add-on".

## Decision

1. **IAM is permission-only and knows no segments; segment targeting is business logic in the rules engine; segments
   is an add-on with its own permissions.** (Owner, 2026-10-28.)
   - **IAM.** An audience of a deny, visibility or field rule is a scope node or a role: `Audience = ScopeRef |
RoleRef`. `Audience$` refuses `{ kind: "segment" }`. `PolicySnapshot` carries no segment memberships, and the
     decision point and the visibility filter match no segment. `@stackra/iam` does not depend on
     `@stackra/segments`. `Permissions`, `Permission` and `Risk` stay in `@stackra/iam`; nothing moves to
     `@stackra/actions`.
   - **Rules.** A rule that targets a segment ("VIP customers cannot cancel an order") is a business rule, a
     `@stackra/rules` condition on a fact: `{ field: "segments.ids", operator: "includes", value: "seg_…" }`. The
     engine gains one small port, `FactProvider` (`source`, `factsOf(subject)`), and `factsFrom(providers, subject)`,
     which gathers each bound provider's facts under its source key. It completes the existing registration hook,
     `Rules.register` ([0455](/adr/0455-rules-pack-defaults-without-a-scope-registered-fact-sources-list-floors-and-sections-without-a-base-pack)), which already lets a later package
     add a source's description. `@stackra/rules` never depends on an add-on and still loads no facts in `evaluate`.
   - **Segments.** `@stackra/segments` is an optional add-on above `@stackra/iam` and `@stackra/rules`. It depends on
     both; neither depends on it. It declares `SegmentPermissions`, whose `recompute` is system-held
     (`Permission.L1.system()`), so `RecomputeSegmentsAction` derives `segments.segment.recompute` and gen's
     `permission_unknown` passes. Every action segments adds later declares its permission there. It supplies the
     `SEGMENT_FACTS` source and `SegmentFactProviderDriver`, the rules `FactProvider` driver that reads materialised
     membership.
   - **Every action has a permission.** gen's `permission_unknown` keeps refusing an action without one, whatever its
     actors. A job's permission is system-held. The OS `traffic.hit.record` case stays a product refusal and takes a
     `Permission.L0` static.

   Rejected options:

## Consequences

- Every action has a permission and a risk. The 40 job-only actions that use a grantable verb today stay valid;
  whether they should move to `.system()` verbs is a product clean-up, not part of this record.
- IAM snapshots get smaller and IAM evaluates no business grouping. A rule stored with a segment audience before this
  record no longer parses (`Audience$`); an older snapshot event's `segments` key is ignored. No such rule ships in
  core, OS or Commerce at the time of writing (the audit found segment audiences only in iam's own tests).
- A consumer that wants a segment-targeted rule registers `SEGMENT_FACTS` on its `Rules` and passes
  `factsFrom(providers, subject)` with its own facts. Without segments installed, the source is absent and such a rule
  is `field_unknown` at save, as any unknown field is.
- The package graph: `@stackra/segments` depends on `@stackra/iam` and `@stackra/rules`; `@stackra/iam` depends on
  neither segments nor anything that reaches it; `@stackra/rules` does not depend on segments or iam. No cycle.
- gen and lint read one table, so a member that passes lint no longer fails gen on placement (STA-1365 items 1 to 4,
  8 and 9). The `oxlint-disable` comments in `container.service.ts` and `action-pipeline.service.ts` go away.
- Someone must keep the table: a new row is an amendment of this record, never a new path pattern in one checker.
- STA-1365 items 5 to 7 (`AreaStorageDriver`, `ProviderDrivers<…>` in documents, `I18nKey`) are gen reading gaps,
  not exemptions, and stay with STA-1365.

## Enforcement

- built — stackra-211 (STA-1368): `Audience` is `ScopeRef | RoleRef` with its exact-set type test and `Audience$` test;
  iam's `package.json` has no `@stackra/segments`; `FactProvider` and `factsFrom` in `@stackra/rules`;
  `SegmentPermissions`, `SEGMENT_FACTS` and `SegmentFactProviderDriver` in `@stackra/segments`. Gated by the testing
  session on landing.
- planned — STA-1365: gen honours rows E1 to E8 and reads the same table as lint.
- planned — STA-1365 or a batch filed for it: `stackra/base-matches-suffix`
  (`packages/developer/coding-standard/src/core/constants/base-matches-suffix-rule.constant.ts`) reads the table in
  place of its `BELOW_CONTAINER` and `TESTING_ENTRY` patterns and its "name ends in the kind" acceptance for errors.
  The home of the shared table must be a package both checks can import without a cycle (rule 6c).
- turbo refuses a package cycle, so an edge back from iam or rules to segments fails every task.
