## Context

`stackra gen` builds a consumer's `#stackra/clients/<service>`, its listener registrations and its read-model types from
the producer's committed `stackra.manifest.json` ([0260](/adr/0260-cross-service-calls-through-injected-clients-and-events)
decisions 2 and 3, [0294](/adr/0294-programming-model-v3-grammar-folders-identities-and-naming) rule R10). Today gen finds that
manifest only in a sibling folder. When no sibling exists it falls back to any dependency whose member name matches, and that
match can pick an unrelated package's manifest. Product gates went green only with uncommitted local copies of the
manifests, so the fallback hid the gap instead of reporting it.

Three ways to ship the manifests across repositories were weighed. A contract package on the registry (chosen) reuses the
registry, the catalogs, OIDC publishing and the dependency lookup gen already has. A `stackra manifests pull` command with
committed copies and a lock file was rejected: it adds a second channel beside the registry, committed copies drift, and it
needs a cross-repo read token. A central manifest repository consumed as a submodule was rejected: one global ref, cross-repo
write tokens, merge races and an informal second registry.

Clients alone do not deliver a cross-app event. [0260](/adr/0260-cross-service-calls-through-injected-clients-and-events)
decision 4 assumes one product, and Encore Pub/Sub topics cannot be shared between apps. So the transport of events that cross
apps is decided here too, and phase 1 ships only the clients.

## Decision

### Contracts

1. **A contract is a package.** A service that another repository consumes publishes `@figentra/<service>-contract`, where
   `<service>` is the service's published name after `@figentra/`, as [0349](/adr/0349-product-scopes-package-names-and-the-module-key-registry-across-products)
   decision 2 names it. The package holds three things and nothing else: `stackra.manifest.json`, `openapi.json` and the
   service's lang catalogue. It carries no source: no `src/` folder and no source maps.
2. **The kind is `contract`.** `stackra.kind` gets a sixth value, `contract` (decision 11 of
   [0453](/adr/0453-the-stackra-registry-one-npm-compatible-store-on-cloudflare-and-our-own-licence-engine), amended below).
   The kind, and not a `-contract` suffix on the name, says what a package is. A contract is not installed by `stackra add`.
3. **Consumers pin through the catalog, and gen reads `node_modules`.** A consuming member declares
   `@figentra/<service>-contract` as a devDependency with `catalog:`, so its version comes from `pnpm-workspace.yaml` as every
   other version does. When no sibling folder holds the service, gen reads the manifest from that package, and only when the
   consumer declares it. The member-name fallback is removed: a service with neither a sibling nor a declared contract is
   refused. A contract is never hosted as a module, kind, driver or package; discovery skips it.
4. **Each producer releases its own contract.** The `release.yml` of the producer repository packs the contract and publishes
   it through GitHub OIDC ([0453](/adr/0453-the-stackra-registry-one-npm-compatible-store-on-cloudflare-and-our-own-licence-engine)
   decision 6, as amended below). No repository publishes another repository's contract.
5. **One trusted publisher per producer repository.** The `@figentra` row of the 2026-10-31 scope table gets one publisher
   per producer repository, bound to the contract names of that repository's own services. The registry refuses a contract
   published by any other repository.
6. **Each contract has its own version line.** A contract is versioned by its service's changesets. A change that breaks the
   manifest or the OpenAPI document is a major version, as decided by `stackra diff` ([0157](/adr/0157-stackra-inspect-and-stackra-diff)).
   A consumer moves by a catalog bump, and `stackra gen --check` reports a consumer whose generated output differs from its
   pinned contract.
7. **Branch builds publish prerelease versions.** A branch or pull request build of a producer may publish a prerelease
   contract version under the dist-tag `pr-<n>` (`n` is the pull request number). A branch build never moves `latest` and
   never publishes a stable version. A branch may pin such a prerelease exactly in its catalog; a build from `main` refuses
   one.
8. **The OS handover manifests go first.** The first contracts published are the OS handover manifests. Once they are on the
   registry, the uncommitted local overlays that stand in for them retire.

### Events that cross apps

9. **Phase 1 ships clients only.** A consumer in another repository gets the generated client from the contract, and its
   calls and queued commands go through the transport of [0142](/adr/0142-cross-service-transport-through-drivers). A listener
   on an event of another repository waits for decisions 10 to 15.
10. **Contracts say what, transport says how.** A contract declares what a service exposes: its actions, queries, events,
    refusal codes and lang catalogue. How an event crosses apps is decided by decisions 11 to 15. Sharing a contract never
    implies sharing a bus, and a consumer with a contract gets no subscription to the producer's Encore Pub/Sub topics.
11. **One envelope for every transport.** An integration event that crosses apps uses the envelope of
    [0141](/adr/0141-integration-event-names-and-envelope) decision 2: its `id` is the event id, `occurredAt` is the
    occurred-at time, `tenant` and `actor` carry the tenant and scope context, and `correlation` is the correlation id.
    The cross-app form adds two fields: `envelopeVersion`, the version of this envelope, and `producer`, the producer's module
    key and repository. The envelope does not change with the transport.
12. **The producer's outbox is the only source, and a relay carries it.** An event leaves a producer only from its
    transactional outbox ([0078](/adr/0078-messaging-layer-eight-packages), [0260](/adr/0260-cross-service-calls-through-injected-clients-and-events)
    decision 5), never from a request handler. A cross-app relay reads the outbox and delivers each event to each subscribed
    consumer, by one of two drivers behind the existing event-bus port of [0142](/adr/0142-cross-service-transport-through-drivers):
    - HTTP push: one signed request per event to the consumer's inbox endpoint, with the signing key read from the secret
      store ([0173](/adr/0173-secrets-infisical-is-the-source-of-production-secrets)), never from a file;
    - a broker driver. No broker is chosen here, and [0227](/adr/0227-rejected-and-deferred-technologies-refreshed) keeps
      Kafka, NATS and Redpanda deferred. The in-service relay of [0260](/adr/0260-cross-service-calls-through-injected-clients-and-events)
      decision 4 is a different thing and stays inside one service.

    This page does not choose between the two drivers. Either one must meet decision 13.

13. **Consumer inboxes are idempotent.** Delivery is at least once. A consumer records each event id in its inbox in the same
    transaction as the handling. A delivery whose id is already recorded is acknowledged and not handled again.
14. **Encore Pub/Sub stays inside one app.** Within one app, events keep the typed subscription of
    [0260](/adr/0260-cross-service-calls-through-injected-clients-and-events) decision 4. Across apps, only decisions 11 to 13 apply.
15. **Gen refuses a remote listener until the relay ships.** A listener on an event of a service with no sibling folder, that is
    contract-only, is refused with `client_event_remote_transport`. The refusal names the event and the service. The refusal
    is lifted when the relay of decision 12 ships.

## Consequences

- A consumer builds against the manifest it declares. A wrong manifest can no longer be picked by name, and a missing one is a
  visible refusal instead of a silent substitute.
- The registry gains a kind, one publisher row per producer repository, and a prerelease path for branches. Each branch build
  adds an immutable version (0453 decision 7), so the registry keeps those versions. Their cleanup is not decided here.
- Each producer repository needs a release job and a publisher row before its contract can be consumed. Each consumer repository
  needs a catalog entry for the contract it uses.
- Contracts follow their service's version line, not the product line of 0349 decision 5.
- Cross-app listeners wait for the relay. Until it ships, a product that needs them stays on calls and queued commands.
- The relay needs an outbox on the producer side, an inbox on the consumer side, and signing keys in the secret store. Those are
  chores for the owner and the lanes that build them.

## Enforcement

- planned — STA-TBD: `stackra gen` reads a contract only from a declared dependency, and refuses a service with neither a
  sibling folder nor a declared contract (`client_service_unknown`), with a test per path in `packages/developer/gen`.
- planned — STA-TBD: gen discovery skips a package whose `stackra.kind` is `contract`, so no contract is hosted.
- planned — STA-TBD: the contract pack step refuses a package that holds a `src/` folder, a source map, or any file outside
  decision 1's three items.
- planned — STA-TBD: the registry refuses a contract from a workflow or repository that is not the publisher row of its name,
  refuses a stable version from a ref other than `refs/heads/main`, and refuses the `contract` kind until its schema lands.
- planned — STA-TBD: `pnpm validate` refuses a prerelease contract in a catalog of the `main` branch.
- planned — STA-TBD: `stackra gen --check` fails when a consumer's generated output differs from its pinned contract.
- planned — STA-TBD: gen refuses a remote listener with `client_event_remote_transport` (decision 15).
- planned — STA-TBD: the relay and its inbox (decisions 12 and 13), with a test that a repeated event id is handled once.
- Nothing checks the handover of decision 8 or the choice of driver in decision 12; they are owner and lane steps tracked in Linear.
