## Context

[0483](/adr/0483-analytics-relay-apps-send-product-analytics-only-to-the-analytics-service) routes every app's
behaviour events through the `relay` driver to the analytics service, which fans out to Figentra's own PostHog and
GA4. It left tenants' own tools open. The owner's answers of 2026-10-30 widen that: tenants at every level of the
recursive tree ([0210](/adr/0210-tenancy-recursive-tenant-tree-lifecycle-one-context-and-region))
connect any analytics or ad tool as a connector of the integrations service; Figentra and the levels above a tenant
must see transactions from everywhere; and no tenant should find its tools short of data without being told why. Most
parts exist. Every vendor connector is OS integrations code ([0443](/adr/0443-integration-architecture-connectors-apps-and-extensions-in-one-marketplace-and-vendor-drivers-in-os-integrations)
decision 7), a tenant's credentials are a `Connection` in the connection store
([0308](/adr/0308-integrations-v3-1-provider-descriptions-connection-stores-and-webhooks)), and `@stackra/integrations`
already has `AdPlatformConnector`, `MatchKeys` hashing and the `ConversionBridge` that turns a business event into a
server conversion. What is missing is the rule for which tenants and tools receive an event, and the guarantees around
it. The tree sets the hard part: today a parent sees a child only through an explicit, audited grant (0210 decision 1,
[0243](/adr/0243-organisations-on-the-tenant-tree-consolidated-billing-guardrails-and-standalone-exit) decision 7).

## Decision

1. **Three flows, kept apart.**
   - _Figentra's product analytics_: behaviour events about the use of Figentra's own product, as 0483 decides.
   - _Tenant analytics_: behaviour events (page and screen views, clicks) and conversions (an order placed, a payment
     captured, a refund) sent to the tools the tenants on the event's route have connected.
   - _Business transactions_: payments, orders and the ledger stay owned by their services. They reach dashboards
     through read models and the warehouse ([0163](/adr/0163-reports-exports-imports-and-declared-read-models), note
     of 2026-10-29), and reach a connected tool only as a conversion built from their integration event.
2. **Roles: the tenant is the controller, and Figentra has a dual role.** The tenant whose surface or sale an event
   belongs to is the controller of its customers' data, and Figentra is its processor. Figentra is also a controller
   for named purposes of its own (platform analytics, fraud, billing and product improvement), as Shopify is, stated
   in the platform terms every tenant accepts at sign-up. The data processing agreement
   ([0189](/adr/0189-privacy-operations-and-data-retention) decision 5) and the record of processing name both. A tool
   a tenant connects is that tenant's own vendor on its instruction, not Figentra's sub-processor.
3. **Who receives an event: its route.** The analytics service works out the route on the server, from records, and
   never from an id in the envelope:
   - **Parties.** The tenant whose surface the event happened on (the request's tenant context), and for a sale the
     seller tenant on the order or order line (a marketplace sale has both).
   - **Organisation ancestors.** Every ancestor of a party that holds it as an organisation member (0243 decision 1)
     receives full detail.
   - **Other ancestors.** A host or reseller ancestor receives what the link's sharing level allows: `detail`,
     `aggregates` (the default) or `none`. The level is set when the child joins (0243 decision 4), accepted by the
     child, shown in the child's settings, and changed only with both sides' approval.
   - **Aggregates are not events.** An ancestor at `aggregates` sees each child's totals in the built-in dashboards and
     its warehouse. Nothing is pushed to its connected tools.
   - **One region.** A route stays inside the event's region
     ([0353](/adr/0353-data-residency-a-region-fixed-at-sign-up-a-login-directory-that-only-routes-and-copies-within-a-jurisdiction)
     decision 5). An ancestor in another region gets aggregates only.

   The organisation membership and an accepted sharing level are the explicit, audited grant that 0210 decision 1 and
   0243 decision 7 require; this amends both.

4. **Figentra sees every event, in its region.** Figentra's own view holds full event-level data for every tenant: a
   platform warehouse and a self-hosted PostHog in each region. Figentra's global tools (PostHog EU and GA4 of 0483)
   receive events without contact data. This amends 0483 decision 5 for transaction events; behaviour events with no
   personal data keep 0483's single-jurisdiction allowance.
5. **Connectors live in the integrations service.** Stackra keeps the ports and machinery, free
   ([0461](/adr/0461-the-pro-line-for-0-1-0-runtime-free-behind-entitlements-paid-studios-and-advanced-drivers)
   decision 1, 0443 decision 8):
   - `@stackra/integrations` gains an `AnalyticsToolConnector` port (`ConnectorKind.Analytics`: `sendEvents`, and an
     optional `erasePerson`).
   - `AdPlatformConnector` gains an optional `erasePerson`.
   - Each connector declares `canErase`: true only when it can erase a person at the vendor with the connection's own
     grant, proven by a recorded case.
   - The vendor connectors (GA4, PostHog, Firebase, Meta, TikTok, Snap, Google Ads and later ones) are OS integrations
     connectors (0443 decision 7). Their connections sit at the tenant level or at a scope below it (0308 decision 9).

   Their secrets are tenant secrets in the connection store: written blind on create or replace, never returned by a
   read, list, export, log, audit entry or error, resolved only by the sending connector at call time
   ([0107](/adr/0107-connections-store-and-three-key-homes) decision 4), and destroyed on disconnect.

6. **The analytics service routes; the integrations service sends.**
   - **Routing data.** The analytics service keeps a projection of the connections on each scope. It is fed by the
     integrations service's connection events (connector key, kind, owning scope, consent category, `canErase`,
     `phoneFormat`; never a secret), plus the sharing levels fed by tenancy.
   - **Routing.** For each verified batch (0483 decision 2), and for each business integration event mapped to a
     conversion, it computes the route, applies decisions 7 and 8 per connection, and publishes one
     `AnalyticsDeliveryRouted` (`analytics.delivery.routed.v1`) per connection.
   - **Sending.** The integrations service handles each one in the connection owner's context, through the connector.
     Each connection retries alone. `ConversionBridge` takes routed conversions instead of reading business events
     itself.
   - **Secrets.** A secret never leaves the integrations service.

   Stackra's identifiers speak of a scope through `ScopeRef`, never of a tenant
   ([0323](/adr/0323-tenant-vocabulary-lives-only-in-tenancy-and-scopes-use-the-scoperef-port)).

7. **What each connection receives.**
   - **Behaviour.** The 0483 envelope, unchanged: event name, declared properties, anonymous id, session id and public
     user id. It never carries a contact field, an IP address, a user agent or an advertising id.
   - **Conversions.** Name, value, currency, items, the order's public id as the event id (so a pixel and a server
     send count once), and the ad click id when `marketing` is granted.
   - **Match keys.** Hashed email, phone and external id (`matchKeysOf`, the connection's `phoneFormat`). Computed in
     memory by the analytics service and never stored raw. Sent only to a connection whose connector declares
     `canErase`, and only when the event's consent grants `marketing`. An audience upload also needs the person's
     stored proof ([0472](/adr/0472-server-side-consent-a-signed-short-lived-consent-snapshot-travels-with-server-calls)
     decision 5).
   - **Classification.** Match keys are a classified field ([0134](/adr/0134-data-invariants-classification-and-delete-lifecycle))
     of the routed event.
8. **Consent per connection.**
   - **Behaviour.** A behaviour event reaches a connection only when the visitor's verified snapshot grants that
     connection's category.
   - **Conversions.** A conversion carries the snapshot of the call that produced it (0472 decision 4).
   - **The banner.** The banner on a tenant's surface names the categories of recipients on its routes, including
     ancestors that receive detail and their tools. A change to that set changes the surface's consent policy version,
     so visitors are asked again ([0298](/adr/0298-consent-and-analytics) decision 3).
9. **Erasure and retention.**
   - **Who got what.** The integrations service keeps a recipients ledger: for each subject's public id, the
     connections that received its match keys or user id, and when.
   - **Erasing.** On `SubjectErased` it calls `erasePerson` on each of them and lists each on the erasure receipt
     (0189 decision 2). Only connectors that can erase ever receive contacts, so the erasure is complete. Connections
     that only received behaviour hold anonymous ids we cannot tie to the person.
   - **Replay store.** Kept encrypted in the region for the replay window, and purged by erasure.
   - **Offboarding.** On offboarding (0189 decision 3) a tenant's connections and sharing levels are deleted. A child
     that leaves its organisation (0243 decision 5) stops routing upward from that moment.
10. **Delivery guarantees, so a tenant always knows why.**
    - **Health page.** One page per connection, in the owner's console: events sent, dropped by consent (with the
      reason), sent without match keys (with the reason), failed, and the last error. It is fed by the integrations
      service's send outcomes and an hourly `AnalyticsRouteSummarised` of the analytics service's drops.
    - **Replay window.** Delivered events are kept encrypted in the region for a set number of days. The tenant can
      re-send a time range after fixing a key or an outage, with the same event ids. The console warns where the
      vendor does not dedup, such as GA4's Measurement Protocol.
    - **Alerts.** The tenant's admins are notified after repeated failures or a rejected credential.
    - **Reconciliation.** Each day, per tenant and connection, the ledger's orders and revenue (analytics-reports
      read models) are set against what was routed and what was sent, with the difference broken down by reason.
11. **Every region, lockable.** Connectors are available in every region's stack and run in the region of the scope
    that holds the connection. A deployment may lock a connector off per region or per plan
    ([0108](/adr/0108-scoped-settings) S2). The transfer to the vendor is the controller's choice, and the console
    states it before the tenant connects.
12. **Not the built-in dashboards.** analytics-reports' dashboards and the warehouse
    ([0190](/adr/0190-seed-profiles-and-product-analytics) decision 3, 0163) read business data, stay in the region and
    are served by us. They are also where `aggregates` ancestors see their children. Connected tools receive only the
    routed events of decisions 7 and 8. Neither replaces the other.

## Consequences

- One rule answers "who gets this event" at every depth of the tree, and the health page and reconciliation explain
  every gap a tenant sees.
- The analytics service carries the routing for the whole platform: per batch, one routed event per connection on the
  route. The projection and the route computation must be cheap. The integrations service carries the sends, the
  recipients ledger and the replay store.
- Organisation parents and Figentra become recipients of child and tenant data. The platform terms, the DPA and the
  organisation join flow must say so before release; legal review is the product's
  ([0071](/adr/0071-privacy-obligations) decision 5).
- Tools that cannot erase, such as GA4 through the Measurement Protocol, never get contacts, so their ad matching is
  weaker. The health page says this.
- Figentra needs a self-hosted PostHog and a platform warehouse in each region.
- Amends [0210](/adr/0210-tenancy-recursive-tenant-tree-lifecycle-one-context-and-region) decision
  1 and [0243](/adr/0243-organisations-on-the-tenant-tree-consolidated-billing-guardrails-and-standalone-exit)
  decision 7 (decision 3), [0483](/adr/0483-analytics-relay-apps-send-product-analytics-only-to-the-analytics-service)
  decision 5 for transaction events (decision 4), and the `ConversionBridge` input (decision 6).

## Enforcement

planned:

- `@stackra/analytics/node` tests: a route is built only from the request context and records; an organisation
  ancestor gets detail; a host at `aggregates` gets nothing; a route never crosses a region; match keys only reach a
  `canErase` connection with `marketing`; one routed event per connection.
- `@stackra/integrations` tests: `canErase` connectors erase every subject in the recipients ledger; a secret never
  appears in a read model, log or error.
- The tenancy isolation test: a tenant outside the route never receives an event.

Linear: STA-1628.
