## Context

Some actions need a stronger proof of identity than a session. The owner's answers I5, I6 and SC14 (mem L609, L610, L553, 2026-10-20) fix the step-up rule, the machine identity types, and the ASVS baseline.

## Decision

1. **MFA policy (I5).** An MFA policy cascades down the tenant tree. The authentication assurance level (AAL) is carried in the token. A level L3 or L4 action needs a step-up within 10 minutes. Without it, the refusal is `step_up_required`.
2. **Machine identities (I6).** All machine identities go through ZITADEL: service accounts, personal access tokens (free), tenant service tokens (an entitlement tier), OAuth apps, and the device flow for devices (ADR 0235).
3. **Baseline (SC14, ASVS level 1).** MFA for administrators and operators. A breached-password check. Sessions last 30 days, or 12 hours on the platform. Rate limits and Turnstile.

## Consequences

- A destructive action cannot run on an old session, because a step-up is required within the window.
- Tenant service tokens are a plan tier, so a machine credential is part of the commercial model (ADR 0242).

## Enforcement

The pipeline checks a user caller's assurance against the action's `stepUp` (ADR 0406 as amended 2026-11-03); gen
refuses an L3 or L4 action a user can call with no `stepUp`, `step_up_missing` (`checkCallShape`, STA-340). The ASVS
level of each security-relevant member is declared in its threat model and checked by `stackra validate`
(amendment of 2026-11-26).

## Amendment 2026-11-26 (owner decision 2026-11-06): the ASVS level

The owner accepted the gap analysis' recommendation P1-1 on 2026-11-06 (STA-204): Stackra targets OWASP ASVS **level 2**
for authentication, sessions and access control, before general availability. This amendment records it and adds it
to decision 3. STA-316.

- **Decision 3, as amended.** The baseline is ASVS level 1 for every chapter, and **level 2** for ASVS 4.0.3 chapters
  V2 (authentication), V3 (session management) and V4 (access control). A member that authenticates, holds a session
  or decides access (`@stackra/identity`, `@stackra/iam`, `@stackra/scope`, `@stackra/tenancy`, `@stackra/actions`
  and `@stackra/app`) is held to level 2 for those chapters.
- **Version.** Requirement ids in code and docs are ASVS 4.0.3's (`ASVS 2.2.1`, `ASVS 2.7`). Moving to ASVS 5.0,
  which renumbers the chapters, is a separate change that updates every cited id at once.
- **Where it is declared: law SEC10.** This amendment adds a law to [Security](/constitution/security), because a
  level nobody declares per member is never checked: "Every security-relevant member keeps `docs/threat-model.md` in
  the template, opening with its OWASP ASVS level and its review date, and the platform model links every one. The
  level is L2 for authentication, sessions and access control and L1 for every other chapter. A change that adds an
  asset, a trust boundary or an external call updates the member's model in the same pull request." A member is
  security-relevant when it authenticates or authorises a caller, holds a secret or credential, keeps personal data,
  verifies third-party input, or runs code or markup it did not write: every member under `packages/security/` and the
  members `THREAT_MODEL` of `@stackra/repo-checks` names. The platform model is [Threat model](/customers/threat-model).
- **Enforcement.** `stackra validate` checks each model's shape, the ASVS and review lines and that a threat with no
  control names its issue (`threat-model`), and that the platform page links every required member
  (`threat-model-index`). Whether a listed threat is real or a control works stays review.
- **What it is not.** A declared level is the target the controls are built and reviewed against. It is not a
  verification: no third party has tested Stackra against ASVS yet (the penetration test is STA-1321).

Decisions 1 and 2 are unchanged.
