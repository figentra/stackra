Every incident gets a postmortem within five business days of being resolved, at every level
([ADR 0185](/adr/0185-incident-response-on-call-status-page-and-support-tiers) decision 2). A SEV1 or SEV2 gets the
full template below; a SEV3 or SEV4 may use the short form (summary, timeline and actions) in its Linear issue. The
incident's issue stays open until the postmortem is linked and every corrective action is filed.

## Blameless

A postmortem looks for the gap in the system or the process that let a person's ordinary action cause harm. It never
names a person as the cause. People are named only as actors on the timeline, and an unexplained change is a question
to answer, not a finding about who made it. The test of a good postmortem: someone else on a bad day could not repeat
the incident the same way.

## Who writes it

The incident commander owns it. The `incident-commander` agent can draft it from the timeline on the Linear issue and
the evidence (logs, deploy records, `git log`); the incident commander checks every line of the draft against the
evidence before it is reviewed.

## The template

Attach it to the incident's Linear issue as a document named `Postmortem: <slug>`.

```markdown
# Postmortem: <slug>

- **Severity:** SEV1 | SEV2 | SEV3 | SEV4 (and any change during the incident)
- **Class:** outage | degraded | data loss | security | personal-data breach | pipeline | release
- **Detected / Mitigated / Resolved (UTC):** <t> / <t> / <t>
- **Detected by:** uptime monitor | heartbeat | Sentry | customer | person
- **Impact:** <what broke, for whom, how many, for how long; data lost or exposed, if any>
- **Root cause:** <one paragraph: a system or process gap, never a person>

## Timeline

| UTC | Event | Actor |
| --- | ----- | ----- |

## What went well

## What went badly

## Where we got lucky

## Contributing factors

- <factor>: could a check have refused this? <yes: which / no: why>

## Corrective actions

| Issue | Action | Owner | Enforced by a check? |
| ----- | ------ | ----- | -------------------- |

## Runbook changes

- `apps/docs/src/docs/runbooks/<slug>.mdx`: <added / amended>
```

The times give the two numbers worth tracking across incidents: time to detect (start of impact to detection) and time
to mitigate (detection to the end of customer impact).

## Corrective actions

- **Each action is a Linear issue** linked from the postmortem, with an owner and a priority. An action without an
  issue is not an action.
- **Prefer a check to a promise.** A precondition written only in prose is not a precondition. For every contributing
  factor ask which test, lint rule, gate or alert would have refused it, and file that.
- **Fix the runbook in the same week.** If a step was missing, wrong or hard to find, amend the page and list it under
  "Runbook changes". A new runbook goes in the Runbooks group of `apps/docs/src/config/docs.config.ts`.
- **A decision the incident forces is the owner's.** Write it up as a question for the owner; an ADR records the
  answer.

## Review

The owner reads every SEV1 and SEV2 postmortem and agrees the actions and their priority. When a second person joins,
the review is a short meeting with whoever took part. The incident's issue closes once the postmortem is linked and its
actions are filed, not when the actions are done.

## Publishing

Postmortems are internal. For a SEV1 or SEV2 that customers saw, the incident commander may post a short public
summary on the status page: what happened, how long, what we changed. It names no customer, no person and no
environment detail ([ADR 0185](/adr/0185-incident-response-on-call-status-page-and-support-tiers) decision 4). A
security incident or a personal-data breach is never summarised in public beyond what notification requires
([Incident response: Personal-data breaches](/runbooks/incident-response#personal-data-breaches)).
