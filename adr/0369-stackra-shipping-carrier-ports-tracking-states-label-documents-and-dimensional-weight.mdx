## Context

Commerce moved shipping into release 1 (COM-37) and asked for one new Stackra package, `@stackra/shipping`
(`notes/handover/stackra-package-needs.md`, Commerce `services-and-packages.md`). The answers it serves: main carriers
per country plus one aggregator (COM-147); the merchant's own or the platform's carrier accounts (COM-148); flat, table
and live rates (COM-149); A4 PDF and 4x6 thermal ZPL labels with batch printing (COM-153); customs data for cross-border
parcels (COM-154); dimensional weight for box choice and cost (COM-155); pickup points through connectors that offer
them (COM-156); carrier events normalised into one set of tracking states (COM-158); and a failed-delivery flow
(COM-159). The carrier itself is an Integration with a Connection, and which carrier a tenant uses in a Market is the
rule of [0356](/adr/0356-choosing-a-provider-per-tenant-and-market-through-the-connection-with-a-market-default). Nothing
on disk defines a carrier contract.

## Decision

1. **One package, mechanism only.** `@stackra/shipping` (`packages/commerce/shipping`, `tier:lib`) holds the carrier
   contract and the arithmetic every consumer shares. Carrier and aggregator drivers are OS integrations connectors;
   zones, rates, free shipping, restrictions, the delivery promise, slots, box suggestion and the failed-delivery flow
   are the Commerce shipping module with rule sets and workflow templates
   ([0363](/adr/0363-configuration-over-code-business-behaviour-is-data-rules-workflows-and-studios) decision 2).
2. **Capability interfaces on the Connection.** A carrier connection is a `CarrierGateway` (connection id, provider
   code, declared `capabilities`). Each `CarrierCapability` names one interface: `rates` (`RateGateway.quote`),
   `labels` (`LabelGateway.createLabel` and `cancel`), `pickups` (`PickupGateway.schedulePickup` and `cancelPickup`)
   and `tracking` (`TrackingGateway.track` and its `trackingMap`). `hasCapability` narrows by the declared list and
   never calls the carrier. Parcels are whole millimetres and grams; parties and addresses carry what a label prints;
   customs lines carry HS code, origin, quantity, value and weight. This mirrors the gateway shape of
   [0357](/adr/0357-payments-architecture-ports-gateway-drivers-a-platform-layer-and-a-closed-loop-wallet) decision 2.
3. **`CarrierGateways` picks the carrier.** The port answers one gateway for a Market and the capabilities a call
   needs, every such gateway preferred first (live quotes at checkout), or the gateway of a stored connection (cancel,
   pickup and tracking go to the account that bought the label). Its driver applies 0356 decisions 2 and 3 and lives
   with the Connection store in OS integrations; which carriers a Market offers is market-pack data. No match is the
   refusal `no_carrier`. Label and pickup calls carry the caller's `reference`, and a repeated reference returns the
   result already made, so a retry never buys a second label.
4. **One tracking-state model.** `TrackingState` has ten codes (`label_created`, `in_transit`, `out_for_delivery`,
   `ready_for_pickup`, `delivered`, `delivery_failed`, `returning_to_origin`, `returned`, `cancelled`, `exception`);
   `delivered`, `returned` and `cancelled` are terminal. A connector ships a `TrackingMap` from its carrier's codes to
   these states as data. `normaliseTracking` maps polled and webhook events alike, keeps one event per code and
   instant, orders by instant, takes the state of the latest, and lists codes the map lacks instead of guessing them.
   What a shopper is told on a state is a notification rule or workflow, not this package.
5. **Label documents are checked.** A label is `{ format, bytes }` in `pdf` or `zpl`. A connector builds each one with
   `labelDocument`, which decodes base64 and checks the envelope (`%PDF-`; `^XA` … `^XZ`), refusing with
   `label_document_invalid`. `batchZpl` joins ZPL labels into one print job. The format and label stock come from the
   location's printer setting in each request. Printing ZPL is `@stackra/peripherals`' `LabelPrinter`
   ([0339](/adr/0339-point-of-sale-peripherals-printers-labels-drawer-scanner-display-and-the-native-bluetooth-driver)
   decision 1); storing labels and merging PDFs is `@stackra/documents`.
6. **Dimensional weight takes the carrier's numbers.** `dimensionalWeight(parcel, factor)` is the volume over the
   carrier's factor in cubic centimetres per kilogram, in whole grams rounded up; `chargeableWeight` is the greater of
   actual and dimensional weight, rounded up to the carrier's billing increment. Factor and increment come from the
   connector or rate card; the package has no default for either.

## Consequences

- A new carrier is a connector with a tracking map and its factor, not a change here; a new market is pack data.
- Notifications, reports and rules read one state model, so they do not change per carrier.
- OS integrations owns the carrier connectors, the `CarrierGateways` driver and the alert on `unmapped` codes.
- The Commerce shipping and returns modules depend on this package for labels, pickups and tracking.

## Enforcement

planned — the package's unit tests (`packages/commerce/shipping/src/**/__tests__`) cover capability narrowing, the
tracking order and deduplication, the label envelopes and the weight arithmetic with arbitrary factors; the 0363 lint
rule flags a carrier name, factor or tracking code literal in package source; a conformance test per carrier connector
(a repeated reference returns the same shipment; every tracking code in its fixtures is in its map) belongs to OS
integrations.

## Open questions

1. **Pickup points and lockers (COM-156).** Searching a carrier's pickup points near an address is not in the port
   yet; whether it is a fifth capability or a geography concern is not decided.
2. **Shipping resale (COM-148).** Billing merchants for labels bought on the platform's accounts needs an OS ADR note
   and counsel (package-needs Commerce conflict 2); the port is the same either way.
3. **Return labels.** A return label may be a `LabelRequest` with the parties swapped or a carrier's own return
   product; not decided until the returns module is specified.
