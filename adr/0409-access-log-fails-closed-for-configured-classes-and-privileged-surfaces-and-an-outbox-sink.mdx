## Context

`AccessLogService` always failed open: a read was served when its access record could not be written. That is right
for most personal data, but a financial or health read served unlogged breaks the evidence a regulator asks for. The
read audits also stayed in each service's own table, so the audit service never saw them.

## Decision

1. **Fail closed by class, as configuration.** `AccessLogConfig.failClosed` lists the data classes whose read is
   refused (`access_log_write_failed`, retryable) when its record cannot be written; the default is `financial` and
   `health` (the regulated special category). A market pack or deployment changes the list in runway.
2. **A privileged surface fails closed whatever its class**: `@AccessLogged({ failClosed: true })` on break-glass,
   impersonation or staff export reads.
3. **The record decides before the value leaves**: an async method resolves only after its record is written. A
   synchronous method cannot be held back; a fail-closed read is written async.
4. **An `outbox` sink.** `OutboxAccessLogSinkDriver` sends each record as `AccessRecorded`
   (`access_record.recorded`) through the outbox; the audit service chains and stores it.
5. **One canonical hash.** The chain hash is `@stackra/support`'s `canonicalHash` (ADR 0416), byte for byte the
   earlier form, so stored chains still verify.

## Consequences

- A sink outage now refuses financial and health reads; operators alert on `access_log_write_failed`.
- Services that report to the audit service set `sink: outbox`.
