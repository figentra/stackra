## Context

A consuming service keeps each principal's policy snapshot from the IAM service's events (ADR 0208), but only a memory
store existed, keyed by principal alone, so a principal acting in two tenants had one snapshot. A role could hold exact
keys or wildcards (wildcards in system roles only), so a custom role "read everything in orders" listed every key by
hand and fell behind each new permission.

## Decision

1. **Snapshots are tenant-scoped.** `PolicySnapshotStore.read` and `write` take the tenant; `StorePolicySourceDriver`
   reads under the actor's tenant. A platform principal is its own partition (`-`).
2. **The durable store is `iam.policy_snapshots` in Postgres** (`./node`, driver `postgres`): one row per tenant and
   principal, under the forced equality policy on `app.tenant_id`, replaced only by a higher version in one upsert.
3. **The listener body is `PolicySnapshotIntakeService`**: it decodes the untrusted payload and stores it under the
   event's `meta.tenantId`. The listener kind stays in the consumer, because iam does not depend on events.
4. **Selectors.** A role may hold `@read` (every registered L0 and L1 permission) or `@risk:<l0-l4>`, optionally behind
   a module or model prefix (`orders.@read`). A selector counts in any role because it never picks a system or
   restricted permission for a grant; a deny's selector picks them too, since a deny only narrows.

## Consequences

- Each consuming service runs the iam migration and binds `postgres` for `PolicySnapshotStore`.
- Custom roles stay data: an admin grants `orders.@read` and new read permissions are covered on registration.
