## Context

Egypt is priced on a USD list and collected in EGP (R3-SO-14); the US and EU launch from the UAE company until local
companies open (R3-SO-15, US-144). Price lists were keyed by currency alone and nothing said who sells or which
numbering series an invoice uses. Market packs already hold dated values, so a switch of seller on a given day is a
pack entry, not code.

## Decision

1. **Price-list key.** A plan price list is keyed by (regional tier, price currency) (`priceListKey`); the regional
   tier and the price currency of a billing country are market-pack values.
2. **Separate currencies.** `market-pack:<code>` carries `billing.price_currency` and `billing.collection_currency`
   separately, and `billing.rate_source` (the `RateSource` driver key) whenever they differ. Missing values, or
   differing currencies with no rate source, are refused: billing never guesses. The rate snapshot is stored on the
   invoice by OS billing.
3. **Seller and series.** `billing.contracting_entity` and `billing.billing_seller` are dated pack values. The invoice
   series is one per billing seller and market (`<seller>:<market>`), so a new local company opens its own gapless
   series from its first day. `billingTermsOf` reads all of it from `valuesOn` for the invoice date
   (`BILLING_PACK_KEYS`).
4. **Optional cash rounding.** A cash rounding row may be `optional` (the US 5-cent rule, US-39); `roundCash` applies it
   only where the tenant turned it on.
5. **Live rate drivers** (the central-bank and metal-rate feeds) are drivers of the existing `RateSource` port, built
   with their vendor feed; they are not part of this decision.

## Consequences

OS billing reads one function for a country's terms and numbering. The packs need the `billing.*` entries before
launch; the base packs own them.

## Enforcement

Unit tests of `billingTermsOf`, `planPrice` and `roundCash`. planned — a market-pack data test that every launch pack
sets every `BILLING_PACK_KEYS` entry.
