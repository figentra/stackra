## Context

[0239](/adr/0239-rls-shape-equality-on-tenant-id-with-read-models-for-org-views) gives every business table one
row-level-security policy: equality on `tenant_id`, the same for every table, so the isolation test is one pattern. As
the database package is built, that is one permissive policy per table. New needs want rows narrowed further inside a
tenant: a device session that holds only its legal entity's records
([0338](/adr/0338-offline-queue-completed-per-device-order-sealed-payloads-bounded-replicas-revoke-and-caps) decision 3),
and a legal entity as the transfer boundary of a multi-country tenant
([0343](/adr/0343-cross-tenant-record-sharing-consented-copies-fed-by-events) decision 4). Postgres combines policies
two ways: permissive policies are joined by OR, so a second permissive policy widens access; restrictive policies are
joined by AND, so each one can only narrow. The framework build asked on 2026-10-24 how the two layers are used, and
this ADR answers.

## Decision

1. **Exactly one permissive policy per business table, the tenant equality of 0239.** It is named
   `pol_<table>_tenant_id` ([0231](/adr/0231-naming-and-casing-standard)), it is `FOR ALL`, and its one allowed extra
   clause is the audited operator grant of 0239 decision 3. Gen writes it from `BelongsToTenant`
   ([0321](/adr/0321-the-trait-inventory-owning-packages-and-schedulable)); nobody writes a second permissive policy,
   and a migration that creates one fails.

2. **Every narrowing is a restrictive policy.** Anything that limits rows inside a tenant is `AS RESTRICTIVE`, so it
   can only remove rows the tenant policy allows and never add one. Restrictive policies are generated, never
   hand-written, and each is named after the column it tests, `pol_<table>_<column>` (0231).

3. **A model declares the columns a session may be narrowed to.** A model lists them as `rowScopes` (today
   `legalEntityId`, the dimension 0338's device sessions and 0343's transfer boundary use), and gen emits one
   restrictive policy per listed column of the shape "the session setting is unset, or the column equals it":
   `current_setting('app.<column>', true) IS NULL OR <column> = current_setting('app.<column>', true)::<type>`. An
   unscoped session therefore sees the whole tenant, and a scoped session sees its slice. The settings are written by
   the tenancy context (`withTenant`) from the authenticated session, as `app.tenant_id` already is, never from a
   client value.

4. **What is not a restrictive policy.** Record visibility rules
   ([0333](/adr/0333-record-visibility-rules-who-can-see-which-records) decision 5) stay in the query layer; the IAM
   scope path stays a predicate inside one tenant (0239 decision 4); soft deletes, holds and classes are column
   effects, not policies. Read models keep the shape of 0239 decision 2, and a global model
   (`Model.global`, [0210](/adr/0210-tenancy-recursive-tenant-tree-lifecycle-one-context-and-region) as amended on
   2026-10-24) has no policy at all.

5. **Force and audit.** Every tenant-owned table has row-level security enabled and forced. The RLS catalogue audit of
   0239 checks, per table: `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, exactly one permissive policy with the tenant shape, every other
   policy restrictive and named by its column, and no policy that names a column the model does not list in
   `rowScopes`. A policy the audit cannot classify fails the gate.

## Consequences

- Tenant isolation stays one pattern and one test; narrowing cannot widen it, by the semantics of the layer it lives in.
- A scoped device or user is narrowed in the database, not only in the application, so a bug above the database cannot
  leak another legal entity's rows to it.
- Each restrictive policy adds a predicate to every read of its table; a model lists a scope column only when a session
  is actually narrowed to it.
- The migration generator gains the `AS RESTRICTIVE` form and the per-column names, and the frozen-snapshot rule of
  [0159](/adr/0159-migrations-gen-ordered-sql-and-safety-rules) applies to them as to every other statement.

## Enforcement

- planned — the RLS catalogue audit of 0239, extended as decision 5 says, in the tenant isolation tests.
- planned — gen (STA-340): a second permissive policy, a hand-written `CREATE POLICY`, or a restrictive policy on a
  column outside `rowScopes` fails the build; the policy names follow 0231.
- planned — a tenant isolation test: a session with `app.legal_entity_id` set reads no row of another legal entity in
  the same tenant, and a session without it reads the whole tenant.

## Alternatives

- **Widening the tenant policy with more clauses.** Not chosen: every added clause is a chance to widen, and the audit
  can no longer check one shape.
- **Narrowing only in the application layer.** Not chosen for session scoping: the database is the isolation boundary
  of 0239 and 0333 decision 5, and a scoped device must be narrowed there.
- **One schema per legal entity.** Not chosen: 0210 decision 7 keeps the pool with no schema per tenant, and a legal
  entity is a dimension inside a tenant.
