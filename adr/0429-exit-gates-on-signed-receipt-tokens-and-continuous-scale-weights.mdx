## Context

A self-checkout lane checks that the bag holds what was scanned, and lets the shopper out through a gate only with a
paid receipt. The `Scale` port could only be read once, and nothing modelled a gate.

## Decision

1. **`AccessController` port** (`peripherals.AccessController`): `probe()`, `onCredential(listener)` for every credential
   the gate's reader presents, and `open()` for one passage. It decides nothing. Only the `memory` driver ships; a gate
   controller's driver comes with its own ADR under 0253 decision 3.
2. **Signed receipt tokens.** A receipt's QR code carries `base64url(claims).base64url(Ed25519 signature)`, the claims
   being `receiptId`, `nodeId` and `issuedAt`. The till or the receipts service signs with a private key it keeps as a
   secret; a gate holds only the public key, `peripherals.exitTokenPublicKey` (configuration, not a secret).
   `signReceiptToken`, `verifyReceiptToken` and `importReceiptPublicKey` use Web Crypto only.
3. **`ExitGateService`** opens the gate once for a token that verifies, was issued in the gate's node within
   `peripherals.exitTokenMaxAge` (30 minutes by default, a venue setting), has not opened this gate before, and that
   the product's rule set admits (`admit`); it reports every decision with its reason and never throws on a bad scan.
   One gate remembers the last 10,000 receipts; a venue with several exits needs a shared used-receipt store (later).
4. **Continuous weights.** `Scale.onWeight(listener)` streams every weight change, settled or not, until stopped.

## Consequences

- An exit gate works offline: verifying needs no call to the server.
- A leaked private key lets anyone mint exit tokens: the key is rotated through the secret store, and the gates take
  the new public key by configuration.
