## Context

Onboarding is a form document (0279 decision 2), but `@stackra/builder` had no form kind, so onboarding answers were a
typed input. Automations had no editor kernel: the block editor edits trees, and automations and forms are a step list
and a field list.

## Decision

1. **The form kind.** `FormDocument$`: a key `<module>.<name>`, a version, a lang-key label, the action key it submits
   to (`forms.submit@1` by default) and 1 to 20 steps of 1 to 50 fields. A field type is one of `FormFieldType` (text,
   long text, email, phone, number, money, date, yes or no, choice, multiple choice); the registry of types is code,
   the forms are documents. `checkForm` is the write-time check; `formAnswersSchema(form, now)` is the answers' zod
   schema for submit (and, `partial`, for save and resume), shared by server and client.
2. **Conditions are rule-engine nodes.** A step or field may carry a `@stackra/rules` condition over `answers.<field>`:
   a step's over earlier steps, a field's over its own and earlier steps. Money fields are not testable. Hidden answers
   are dropped.
3. **One kernel for structured kinds.** `DocumentEditor` holds a structured document, takes each change as the whole
   next document, keeps undo and redo, and runs the kind's check (`automationProblems`, `formProblems`) without
   throwing; `useDocumentEditor` opens it. The automation editor is this kernel over `@stackra/workflow`'s check and
   building blocks; the host saves through the kind's service, which refuses what the check reports.

## Consequences

- Each product seeds its forms (onboarding first) as documents; the renderer, the submit action and the submission
  model are the experience service's.
- `@stackra/builder` depends on `@stackra/rules` and `@stackra/workflow`.
