## Context

Juristra (JUR-203a), Academorix (A2-24) and the OS (X2-12, J2-6, R3-SO-02) each asked for an AI permission matrix: per
action, role and record (a matter), four states an admin reads as off, suggest, after approval and on its own, a legal
floor from the market pack ("at most after a named lawyer approves"), and an approver who holds the right seat, is not
the requester and has stepped up. The 0360 resolver knew only the platform floor, the organisation's policy and the
user's preferences.

## Decision

1. **One matrix, four states.** `ActionPermission` is `off`, `suggest`, `after_approval`, `on_its_own`. Rows
   (`ActionPermissionRow`) are the one record `rule-set:os:ai-action-permissions`, seeded per product and edited by the
   tenant; a row names an action key and may narrow by role, record (`RecordScope`: a kind, or a kind and an id) and
   data class. The most specific row wins: a record id, then a record kind, then a role, then a data class.
2. **The vocabulary maps once.** Off = the tool is not registered for that role and record (the MCP filter drops it,
   the resolver refuses it). Suggest = propose. After approval = a named approver with the row's approver rule. On its
   own = allow.
3. **The resolver order.** IAM, entitlement, environment; then the matrix's off (refuse) and suggest (propose, below
   every floor because a draft has no effects); then the floors — the platform's and the market pack's, which may also
   list action keys (`ApprovalFloor.actions`) — where a hit or L3 and L4 needs a named approver; then the matrix's after
   approval and on its own; then the organisation's policy, the user's preferences and the default by level.
4. **The MCP filter works by role and record.** `filterTools(tools, access, scope)` asks IAM with the caller's roles
   and record, drops a tool the matrix sets to off there, and lists an unentitled tool with a `locked: true` marker
   (not a separate list), answered "requires subscription".
5. **Propose mode.** `ActionPipeline.propose` runs the steps up to input validation, writes one `usage.recorded` event
   on the `actions.proposals` meter and returns an `ActionDraft` (key, call id, validated input). Approval,
   idempotency, the rate limit, interceptors and `handle` never run.
6. **The approver rule.** `ApproverRule { seatKind, notRequester, stepUp }` is matrix data; `approverRefusal` checks a
   grant against it (seat kind from entitlements, self-approval through the whole on-behalf-of chain, step-up from
   identity) and the `ApprovalGate` driver refuses with the code.

## Consequences

- No product writes its own AI permission engine; Juristra's floor list is pack data.
- `filterTools`' result changes shape (the `locked` list becomes a marker).
- Suggest-mode work is metered like any other usage; its price is the usage service's data.
