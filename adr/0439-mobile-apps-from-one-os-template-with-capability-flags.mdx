## Context

A store binary cannot be shared between brands: each product's staff or customer app needs its own bundle id, icon,
listing and signing. Under [0436](/adr/0436-products-ship-packages-and-figentra-os-ships-the-shells) products write no
app code, so the binaries must come from one OS template with each product's data. Native modules cannot change over
the air, so the template's native set decides what every product can do between store releases. White-label shopper
apps add a third source of builds: a merchant, with or without its own store accounts. Runway's review of 2026-10-26
pointed out that Apple rejects apps that declare permissions they do not use, and that many near-identical apps under
one account meet Apple's spam rule as well as its template-app rule.

## Decision

1. **One template per native line.** The OS mobile template (Expo, `figentra/os/apps/mobile`) carries the union of
   native modules any product may need, each behind a capability flag. Its native line is a runtime version; a native
   change starts a new line.

2. **A product declares its apps as data.** A product's root `runway.yaml` declares each mobile app (template line,
   packages, identity, assets and store paths, capabilities, store accounts, EAS project;
   [0441](/adr/0441-product-repository-layout-assets-tree-and-root-runway-surfaces)). Runway generates the app config
   from the template and that entry, builds on EAS, submits with the listing from `assets/store`, and writes the app-link
   files to the edge ([0437](/adr/0437-the-edge-worker-its-kv-copy-and-the-server-checks)).

3. **Capabilities are removed at build time, not hidden at run time.** A product's build switches on only the
   capabilities it lists. Config plugins driven by `capabilities` leave every other native module, and its permission
   strings and entitlements, out of the binary; nothing is merely hidden at run time. A product that needs a native
   feature the template lacks asks for it in the template, behind a flag. A product's own native app is an exception
   that needs its own ADR (0436 decision 3), the first being
   [0442](/adr/0442-native-android-till-for-pos-terminals-from-the-commerce-till-template).

4. **Releases.** JS, package and document changes ship over the air on the product's channel, as 0180 decision 3 sets.
   A new native line rebuilds and resubmits every product on it.

5. **Offline: the last good update and an embedded fallback.** The mobile shell keeps the last good update and the last
   good documents with a version pin, and starts from the bundle embedded in the binary when no downloaded update
   loads (Expo updates). The OS owns this for the mobile template, as 0436 decision 8 sets.

6. **Identity.** Staff app ids follow `com.figentra.<product>.staff` (the commerce product's is
   `com.figentra.commerce.staff`); customer apps follow `com.figentra.<product>.<app>`. An id is frozen at the first
   store upload.

7. **Store accounts.** A product's own apps publish under one Figentra Apple and one Figentra Google account, as 0180
   decision 1 already says (owner decision of 2026-10-26).

8. **White-label is managed or the merchant's own.** A white-label app (the commerce shopper app) is built from tenant
   data in the OS (brand, identity, listing and the tenant's theme), not from a `brands` list in a repository. A merchant
   with store accounts connects them as Connections (an App Store Connect API key, a Google Play service account) and
   Runway publishes under the merchant; a merchant without them is published under Figentra's account, as other
   regional commerce platforms do (owner decision of 2026-10-26). The build is started by a tenant event, not by a
   commit. Before the first build under Figentra's account, Apple's rules on template apps published for clients
   (guideline 4.2.6) and on many near-identical apps under one account (guideline 4.3, spam) are checked and the
   result is recorded here.

9. **Free components in white-label builds.** A white-label app uses the free `@stackra/ui` only, as 0335 decision 8
   says, whichever account publishes it.

## Consequences

- No product writes Expo config, signing setup or native code; a new product app is a runway entry and assets.
- A native addition is shared work in the OS template and reaches every product on the next line; the config plugins
  that strip unused modules are part of that work.
- Binaries stay small and declare only the permissions they use.
- Runway gains a build started by tenant data and two new Connection kinds (store accounts).
- The Stackra gap "no per-tenant build variants" becomes this ADR's build job.
- The commerce product's native Android till is the first exception ADR under decision 3.

## Enforcement

- planned — Runway refuses a mobile entry whose capabilities the template line does not offer, and an app id outside
  the pattern of decision 6.
- planned — the template's build fails when a module is linked, or a permission string is present, without its
  capability flag.
- planned — the tenant-started job refuses to publish under Figentra's account until decision 8's guideline 4.2.6 and
  4.3 checks are recorded.
